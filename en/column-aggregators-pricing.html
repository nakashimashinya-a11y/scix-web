Prices are set cheapest first

In the balancing market, sellers submit a bid saying “I will sell at this price”. The cheapest bids sell first. If yours sells, the price you bid is what you earn. That is why the bid price has been called the place where skill shows.

That ceiling halved in a year

Fiscal 2025 averages: ¥1 to ¥4

That is because many cheap bids come from thermal plants and the like. Tertiary ② has no cap, yet its average was ¥1.15. In primary reserve from 14 March to 10 April 2026, however, after trading moved to the day before, many bids sold right at the ¥15 cap (ANRE, Document 8).

The high bids came from batteries

Bidding close to the ceiling: seven to nine in ten of such bids came from batteries (April–July 2026). The authorities confirmed this in numbers, and then lowered the ceiling.

Nineteen operators judged to hold large market power have their pricing watched

Under the government guideline, a bid should preferably stay within “the profit that could have been earned in other markets, plus a fixed amount” — it is not an obligation (COLUMN 50). Operators judged likely to hold large market power are checked before trading begins on whether they bid this way. Nineteen companies were named as the proposed scope for fiscal 2026. They were chosen by their share of the market in each area, not by company size.

Hokkaido Electric Power, ENEOS Power, ENERES, Tohoku Electric Power, JERA, E-Flow, TEPCO Energy Partner, TEPCO Renewable Power, Tokyo Gas, Chubu Electric Power Miraiz, Hokuriku Electric Power, Kansai Electric Power, Chugoku Electric Power, Osaka Gas, Shikoku Electric Power, J-POWER, Kyushu Electric Power, Urban Energy, Battery Station Kyushu (Denki no Eki)EGC, Expert Committee on System Design and Market Surveillance, 16th meeting, Document 3 (26 December 2025, secretariat proposal)

The 19 include companies that run other owners’ batteries. Companies outside the 19 are monitored after the fact (COLUMN 50-5).

Trading Rules, Article 41
1

Not failing delivery

Fail to respond after promising, and there is a penalty. For a 30-minute slot in which you fail to follow the dispatch instruction, the penalty equals that slot’s ΔkW revenue. If the same resource fails to follow instructions three times in the same month in the same product, trading in that product can be suspended for that resource. In a case EPRX published in August 2026 (the type of equipment was not disclosed), four failures in April led to a suspension from 21 August. While suspended, that product earns nothing.

2

Not stopping

Battery, communications, control: if any one stops, no bids can be placed. The difference lies in how fast a fault is noticed and how the repair is arranged.

3

Allocating

A battery can also earn in the wholesale market, buying power cheap and selling it dear. Which hours of tomorrow go to which market? The lower the ceiling, the more this judgement counts. Overseas, this is where the gap opened (Overseas).

Does AI help?

We found no material in Japan in which a third party compares the results of companies that use AI with those that do not. There is one way to check: ask for the record in numbers. “Against the market average, at what price did you sell?” “How many penalties and suspensions have there been?”

Frequently asked questions

In Japan’s balancing market, does a higher bid sell for more?

If it sells, the bid price is what you earn. But the cheapest bids sell first, so a high bid is more likely to be left unsold. In products where bids fall short, such as primary reserve, though, bids near the cap have also sold. There is also a cap: ¥10 for primary reserve, secondary ① and the combined product from September 2026.

What is the price cap in Japan’s balancing market now?

From 1 September 2026 it is ¥10 for primary reserve, secondary ① and the combined product, and ¥7.21 for secondary ② and tertiary ① (yen per ΔkW per 30 minutes). Tertiary ② has no cap. Until 13 March 2026 the cap for primary reserve and the others was ¥19.51.

How much does revenue vary with the aggregator’s skill?

In Japan we found no public material comparing results by company, so it cannot be put in numbers. The gap is most likely to show in avoiding penalties and suspensions, keeping the plant online, and allocating between the balancing and wholesale markets.
