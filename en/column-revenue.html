COLUMN 02 — Revenue Structure

3 Revenue Sources for Battery Storage
— JEPX, Balancing Market & Capacity Market Stacking Strategy

Grid-scale battery revenue does not depend on a single market. How you combine three markets determines business success or failure.
In three lines
  1. Grid-scale battery revenue in Japan is built by revenue stacking: layering JEPX arbitrage, the balancing market and the capacity market rather than relying on one market.
  2. On JEPX, batteries charge in cheap 30-minute slots and discharge in expensive ones; in 2024 the average daily high–low spread was about 20 yen/kWh.
  3. The balancing-market cap fell to 10.00 yen from September 1, 2026, while the capacity market pays power sources for simply being there.
Update as of November 2026

Balancing market: the cap for primary reserve, secondary reserve 1 and the composite product was 15.00 yen from delivery on March 14, 2026 and has been 10.00 yen/ΔkW per 30 min since delivery on September 1, 2026 (secondary 2 and tertiary 1 stay at 7.21 yen; tertiary 2 has no cap). In September 2026 (1–29, preliminary, nationwide, our aggregation), average awarded prices were 3.43 yen for primary reserve (online), 2.49 yen for secondary reserve 1 and 2.64 yen for the composite product per ΔkW per 30 min. Capacity market: the Main Auction held in FY2025 (for FY2029 delivery, published January 20, 2026) cleared at 14,972 yen/kW in Hokkaido, 15,111 yen/kW in Tohoku and Tokyo, 12,388 yen/kW in Chubu, Hokuriku, Kansai, Chugoku and Shikoku, and 15,112 yen/kW in Kyushu, for a total of ¥2.2094 trillion after transitional measures. For FY2030 delivery, the OCCTO bidding rules set the bid-acceptance period at October 13–23, 2026 (benchmark price 20,911 yen/kW), with results scheduled for around December 2026.

Battery Revenue Comes from "Three Pockets"

Grid-scale battery revenue is composed of "stacking" — layering three markets rather than relying on a single one. How you design the balance across these three markets is the biggest variable affecting IRR (Internal Rate of Return).

Pocket 1: JEPX Arbitrage (Wholesale Power Market) — Buy low, sell high
Pocket 2: Balancing Market — Selling power "insurance"
Pocket 3: Capacity Market — Fixed income just for "being there"

Pocket 1: JEPX Arbitrage — Price Spread Expanded 5x

The wholesale power market (JEPX) trades in 48 time slots of 30 minutes each across 24 hours. Batteries profit from arbitrage — charging during low-price slots and discharging during high-price slots.

2024 JEPX Price Spread: The average daily spread between highest and lowest prices is approximately 20 yen/kWh
(Around 2020 it was approx. 4 yen/kWh, expanding 5x)

Typical Price Pattern:
- Late night: below 10 yen/kWh
- Daytime with solar surplus: 0.01 yen/kWh (especially frequent in the Kyushu area)
- Evening peak: 20+ yen/kWh

Charge/discharge cycles: 1-2 cycles per day (METI/MRI calculations assume 1-2 blocks per day, with 1 block = 3 hours)
Source: METI Stationary Battery System Expansion Study Group, 4th Meeting Materials / borderless.law 2025 Battery Investment Guide

The massive adoption of solar power has caused daytime market prices to drop to near zero, while evening peak prices spike — the "duck curve" has become the norm in Japan as well. This structure is the source of battery arbitrage revenue.

Pocket 2: Balancing Market — The "Main Battleground" for Batteries, but with Disruption Risk

The balancing market began trading all 5 products (primary reserve, secondary reserve 1&2, tertiary reserve 1&2) in April 2024. The most advantageous for batteries are primary reserve and secondary reserve 1.

Source: METI/MRI Materials (November 11, 2024)

Primary and secondary reserve 1 have extremely high shortfall rates of 70-80% due to insufficient bids, and in the first half of FY2024 it was a "seller's market" in which bids could contract at virtually any price. From FY2025, however, conditions have been improving, and the market environment is changing with the lower price cap (15 yen) and the reduced procurement volume (1-sigma). Battery contract prices of 28-39 yen/ΔkW·h had reached several times thermal's 4.1-8.1 yen.
* Since March 13, 2026, a price cap of 15 yen/ΔkW per 30 min applies, and contracts at that earlier price level are no longer possible.

Warning: Major Risk — Price Cap Reduction from March 2026

Starting from March 13, 2026 transactions, the price cap for Primary, Secondary 1 and the combined product was lowered from the previous 19.51 yen to 15 yen/ΔkW per 30 min (in effect since March 13, 2026; Secondary 2 and Tertiary 1 remain at 7.21 yen). While the initial proposal to unify at 7.21 yen was relaxed due to strong industry pushback, the impact on revenue is significant.

Furthermore, the procurement volumes for primary and secondary reserve 1 were reduced from 3-sigma to 1-sigma equivalent, casting uncertainty on battery business revenue forecasts.
Source: Agency for Natural Resources and Energy (January 23, 2026)
Update: from delivery on September 1, 2026, the cap for Primary, Secondary 1 and the combined product has fallen to 10.00 yen/ΔkW per 30 min. For an estimate of the amount that disappears, by 9 areas × 4 products from EPRX contract results, see balancing market price cap falls to 10 yen.

Pocket 3: Capacity Market — "Stable Income" Reaching Record Highs

The capacity market (Main Auction) conducts auctions 4 years before actual delivery, paying power sources for "simply being there." Batteries can participate with 1,000kW or more, with a win rate of over 96%, meaning virtually all bids are accepted.

5th Main Auction (for FY2028 actual delivery):
Total average unit price: 11,134 yen/kW (record high, after transitional adjustment)
Total contract value: ¥1.8506 trillion (record high)

Contract Prices by Area:
- Hokkaido/Tohoku/Tokyo: 14,812 yen/kW (price cap)
- Kyushu: 13,177 yen/kW
- Chubu: 10,280 yen/kW
- Hokuriku/Kansai/Chugoku/Shikoku: 8,785 yen/kW
Source: OCCTO, published January 29, 2025

Contract prices went from 14,137 yen in the 1st round (uniform nationwide, criticized) → plummeted to 3,495 yen in the 2nd round → then split by area: 5,226 yen in the 3rd → 7,847 yen in the 4th → 11,134 yen in the 5th, continuously rising. The background includes increasing power demand from data centers and semiconductor factories.

However, battery bids in the Main Auction account for only about 0.1% of total volume (approximately 240MW), as most large-scale batteries are flowing toward the LDA.

Revenue Model: 3-Market Stacking for a 2MW Project

METI/MRI has analyzed three use cases. For the capacity market + balancing market combination (Pattern 2), with capacity market revenue of 8,348 yen/kW/year as a base, under conditions of a ΔkW price of 15 yen, 1 block/day, and 60% win rate, the results show IRR is generally positive when construction costs are 70,000 yen/kWh or less.

Aggregator Selection Determines Revenue

Simultaneous participation in three markets requires advanced operational expertise. Many project owners use aggregators (market trading agents).

Major Aggregators:
- Eneres (KDDI subsidiary): Japan's first aggregator. Track record of entering all 5 Balancing Market products.
- Digital Grid: Plans to expand cumulative storage capacity to 383MW by July 2028 fiscal year, investing 10 billion yen.
- PowerX: Manufactures "Mega Power" units of 3MWh each. Adopted in collaboration with Tokyu Land Corporation and others.
- Shizen Energy (Shizen Connect): Operates aggregation services leveraging EMS technology.
Source: Company press releases / pps-net.org

Revenue Structure Differences with LDA-Type (30MW+)

LDA-winning projects over 30MW lock in annual fixed revenue of several hundred million to over a billion yen for 20 years, with approximately 10% of market revenue remaining as additional income. While IRR is 2-4% ("bond-like returns"), the overwhelming predictability of revenue makes project finance structuring much easier.

In contrast, 2 MW-class Full Merchant-type projects may aim for IRR above 10%, while directly bearing regulatory change risks such as Balancing Market price cap reductions.

Frequently asked questions

What is battery revenue stacking?

Revenue stacking means layering income from several markets instead of relying on one. For a grid-scale battery in Japan the three layers are JEPX arbitrage (buy low, sell high), the balancing market (selling power "insurance") and the capacity market (fixed income for being there). How you combine the three markets determines success or failure. Running all three at once takes advanced operational expertise, so many project owners use aggregators.

Which balancing-market products suit batteries in Japan, and what has changed?

The balancing market began trading all five products in April 2024, and primary reserve and secondary reserve 1 are the most advantageous for batteries. Bid shortfalls made it a seller's market in the first half of FY2024, but conditions have been improving since FY2025. The price cap for primary, secondary 1 and the combined product fell to 15 yen in March 2026 and to 10.00 yen/ΔkW per 30 min from delivery on September 1, 2026, and procurement volumes were cut from 3-sigma to 1-sigma equivalent.

How does the capacity market pay batteries in Japan?

The capacity market (Main Auction) holds auctions four years before delivery and pays power sources for simply being there. Batteries of 1,000 kW or more can take part, with a win rate of over 96%. Battery bids in the Main Auction are only about 0.1% of the total (about 240 MW), as most large batteries go to the LDA. The latest clearing prices by area are in the update to this article.
