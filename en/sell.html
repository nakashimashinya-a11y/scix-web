MARKET CONTEXT

Why BESS project rights trade
so actively right now

Total grid interconnection study applications for utility-scale battery storage reached approximately 143 GW as of June 2025, up sharply from about 95 GW just six months earlier. Yet only about 250 MW (0.02% of total) has actually been interconnected, and grid headroom is depleting fast.

At both extra-high-voltage and high-voltage scale, the project economics are the same: the lower the grid connection cost, the higher the profit. Grid-studied project rights and ready-made sites are profit-boosting assets — and they command premium prices.

Extra-high-voltage grid connection costs typically run JPY 2–3B, but well-positioned projects can come in at a few hundred million. Even at high-voltage scale, differences of a few hundred thousand to a few million yen swing project economics. That cost saving flows straight to project profit, which is why buyers nationwide are actively hunting for well-conditioned projects.

VALUATION FRAMEWORK

How distance from the interconnection point
changes value

The single biggest driver of land value for battery storage is the distance to the interconnection point. For extra-high-voltage that's transmission towers and substations; for high-voltage it's distribution substations and lines. Closer means lower connection cost — and higher project value.

Distance to interconnection Indicative connection cost Assessment
Within 300m
TOP TIER 		EHV: JPY 200M – 500M
HV: hundreds of thousands to a few million		 Direct connection to tower / pole-mount transformer is feasible. Maximum cost advantage for buyers — commands top acquisition pricing.
300m – 1km
CONDITIONAL 		EHV: JPY 500M – 1.5B
HV: a few million to tens of millions		 Considerable if a contiguous private-line right-of-way can be secured. Obstacles and third-party land in the path are key.
Over 1km
CASE-BY-CASE 		EHV: JPY 2B+
HV: economically unviable in most cases		 EHV requires new tower construction, sharply raising cost. HV usually cannot recover the cost over the distance.

*These are general guidelines. Actual connection cost contributions are determined by the transmission/distribution operator's interconnection study response. Grid topology, voltage class, and existing facilities can cause significant variation, so we evaluate these holistically in each individual assessment.
