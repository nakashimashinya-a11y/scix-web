LAND SALES COLUMN 08

Battery Development Rights Transfer
— Procedures and Key Considerations

Transferring development rights tied to a connection review response involves different procedures than solar FIT rights sales. Here we organize the practical points.

What Are "Development Rights"?

In the context of grid-scale battery storage, "development rights" is not a legally defined term. In practice, the following elements are bundled together and referred to as "development rights."

Connection review response document. A document issued by the transmission and distribution operator specifying grid interconnection conditions (construction cost contributions, construction timeline, connection point, etc.). This is the core of the development rights.

Land use rights. Ownership or leasehold rights to the project site. The land and the connection review response are linked — if the land changes, the response may become invalid.

Various investigation and permit deliverables. Geological survey reports, boundary survey drawings, environmental assessments, agricultural land conversion permits, development permits, and other deliverables obtained through the development process.

The critical point is that the connection review response defines the conditions for connecting to the grid "at that location, with that capacity." Changing the location or capacity requires a new connection review. In other words, the land and the response only hold value as a set.

Separating "Land" and "Rights"

In development rights transfers, the most important practical consideration is separating the land and the rights.

The land sale is contracted directly between seller and buyer as a real estate transaction. If the Real Estate Brokerage Act applies, a licensed broker may be required.

The rights transfer covers the business rights tied to the connection review response (the applicant's position, succession of various permits, etc.). This portion is generally structured as an "assignment of position" under civil law, rather than as a real estate transaction.

By separating these two contracts, the fair market value of the land as real estate and the business value of the development rights can be evaluated independently.

Transfer Procedures

11-2 weeks

Project Evaluation and Price Agreement

The content of the connection review response, confirmed construction cost contribution amount, legal status of the land, and other factors are evaluated to determine the sale price. Because technical evaluation drives appraisal accuracy, involving someone with practical grid interconnection experience is critical.

21-2 weeks

Execution of Sale Agreements

The land purchase agreement and rights transfer agreement are executed. It is standard practice to include a condition precedent (suspensive condition) allowing termination if the name change is not completed. Payment terms are determined on a case-by-case basis.

33-6 months

Name Change Application to Transmission Operator

An application is filed with the transmission and distribution operator to change the connection review applicant name from the seller to the buyer. The utility reviews whether the connection conditions remain unchanged following the name change. The review period varies by utility and case but is typically 3-6 months.

4After terms agreed

Settlement and Delivery

Payment terms (lump sum, deposit plus balance, etc.) are handled flexibly on a case-by-case basis. Delivery of all documents, land ownership transfer registration, and other steps are scheduled to accommodate the seller's circumstances.

5 Key Considerations

1. Connection Review Response Expiration

Connection review responses have expiration dates; if the construction application is not filed within the deadline, the response lapses. Transfer procedure timelines must be planned backward from this expiration date.

2. Risk of Changed Connection Conditions After Name Change

The transmission operator may re-examine connection conditions when processing a name change. While it is rare for changes to the connection point or capacity allocation to be required, it is prudent to include a condition precedent in the contract requiring that connection conditions be maintained after the name change.

3. Land Title Issues

Mortgage liens, leasehold interests, co-ownership shares, and restrictions on agricultural land title transfers all need to be cleared in advance. In particular, if agricultural land conversion permits are required, confirm whether the permit can be transferred to the new owner.

4. Tax Treatment

Capital gains from the land sale are taxed as real estate transfer income. The tax treatment of gains from the rights portion varies depending on the specific transaction structure, so consultation with a tax advisor is recommended. For corporate sellers, corporate tax applies.

5. Confidentiality

Connection review response documents contain technical information about the grid, and the transmission operator may have imposed confidentiality obligations. Execute a non-disclosure agreement (NDA) with the transferee before sharing any information.

This article provides a general overview of transfer procedures and does not constitute legal advice. For specific projects, please consult with qualified attorneys and tax advisors.
