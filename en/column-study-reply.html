COLUMN 51 Transaction Practice / Reading the Response (Introduction) 2026.10.20

We read connection study responses every day.
These are the six places we look at first

The two lines on construction period and contribution come after. A response is not a promise of connection; its construction period is counted from the date of payment, and it lapses after one year. In two maps: where this paper sits in the flow, and where the six places are among its three papers.

This series has four parts. For those who buy and those who apply themselves, it explains in order how to read a connection study response and an interconnection approval. Parts 2 to 4 are in JapaneseCOLUMN 51　IntroductionCOLUMN 51-2　How to read itJACOLUMN 51-3　ApprovalJACOLUMN 51-4　The numbersJA

Connection study responses reach us every day — those for projects we applied for ourselves and those sent to us by sellers. When a response arrives, the first thing we look at is neither the total construction cost contribution nor the construction period. Neither of those two lines is a firm commitment. The "notes document" that comes with the response says so itself: both the contribution and the construction period are the results of a desk study and may change with the site surveys, land negotiations and outage coordination that follow the contract application. Read the body of the response and two more things become clear. The construction period is counted not from the response date but from the date on which the construction cost contribution is paid, and it does not include the period of the contract procedures. And once one year has passed, counting from the day after the response date, a contract application will no longer be accepted.

We look first at six places: the charging kW and equipment in the application details; the proviso on connection feasibility and the one sentence on the scope of the study; the outline of the works and the reason the connection point was chosen; the rows of the contribution breakdown; the starting point of the construction period; and the notes document. These six places set the outline of whether the project "can be done," at "roughly what cost," and "when" — and they change how the two lines are read.

This series sets out that way of looking, as it is. In this introductory part, we map where the response stands in the flow from pre-consultation to the start of operation, and where the six places lie in a response made of three papers. How to read the six places themselves is in How to read it (51-2)JA; the interconnection approval that arrives afterwards is in Approval (51-3)JA; and the national statistics, the figures from the set of responses we have read (hereafter "our set") and the differences between power companies are in The numbers (51-4)JA.

Who this series is for, and its premises

There are two kinds of reader, and neither is an electricity specialist.

Reader A
Those who buy
A business company or investor that receives a connection study response from the company that developed the project (the seller) and decides whether to buy the project. The figures in the response underlie both the purchase price and the expected connection date.
Reader B
Those who apply themselves
Someone who owns land, applied for a connection study to the power company themselves and has received the response. What to do next, and by when, is written on this paper.

We set three premises. The power company (service area) and the land are decided. The free pre-consultation has been completed. And the project is grid-scale battery storage at high voltage or extra-high voltage. Low voltage follows different procedures; see COLUMN 44.

Terms follow the usage of OCCTO (the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators, Japan). In this series, "response" means the connection study response, and "interconnection approval" means the response (approval) to a contract application. Because document titles differ from company to company, how to tell them apart is placed at the start of How to read it (51-2)JA.

If you hold a project with a connection study response and are weighing whether to let it go, ask for a free sale valuationJA. In our first reply we write whether there is a prospect of a buyer, the form of introduction, and any documents that are missing. The forms we handle and the sequence are in Selling projects and rightsJA.

The terms used in this series

Responses and approvals call the same things by different words. The most confusing is the direction of electricity, which is reversed depending on whether you look from the battery or from the power company. This series uses the following terms.

Where the response sits in the flow

After the pre-consultation, there are five stages to the start of operation. The response is the paper at the exit of stage ①.

The response is an entry paper; at this point there is no seat on the grid yet. The seat gets its place in the queue when the contract application is accepted (connection reservation) and is secured with interconnection approval. From the date of approval three clocks start: a construction cost contribution contract within 1 month, documents on land-use rights within 2 months, and payment by the due date. (The 2-month documents apply to non-FIT and non-FIP projects whose contract applications are accepted on or after October 1, 2026. OCCTO, "The Flow of Grid Access," September 2026 edition.) And the construction period in the response is counted from the date of payment. That is why you end up rebuilding the expected connection date yourself, adding the period of the contract procedures to the construction period in the response.

The skeleton of this flow is set by OCCTO's rules (the Transmission and Distribution Business Guidelines), and it is the same at every power company. What differs between companies is what the papers are called and how attachments are added, the level of contributions and construction periods, and operational matters such as caps on the number of connection studies and payment deadlines. The papers are covered from this introduction through Approval; the levels and operations, in The numbers.

A response is made of three papers

The PDF you receive is made of three parts: a cover letter (Form AP7), the attached connection study response (AP8 for high voltage, AK7 for EHV) and the attached notes document (Form AP9). All three are OCCTO's common forms, and every response we have read (in eight service areas, excluding Hokkaido and Okinawa) carried these form numbers. Of the six places we look at first, five are in the attached response, and the sixth spans the notes document and section "4. Next procedures" of the attachment.

March 10, 2026A

To: Mr./Ms. ○○, General Manager, ○○ Department, ○○ Co., Ltd.

○○, General Manager, ○○ Department, ○○ Electric Power Transmission and Distribution Co., Inc.

The connection study for your application (accepted December 15, 2025: receipt no. ○○○○C) has been completed, and we respond as set out in the attached "Connection Study Response." In evaluating the viability of your business on the basis of these study results, and in arranging the land, generation equipment and other items needed for the generation business, please consider the matters stated in the attached "Notes Document."D

  • A
    The response date. The one-year validity is counted from the day after this date. The date on the cover letter is the most important number.
  • B
    Tell the paper by its subject line. If it reads "Response to your contract application (approval)," it is the interconnection approval, a different paper (Table 1 of How to read it).
  • C
    The acceptance date and receipt number. The number of days from acceptance to the response date shows whether the response came within the 3-month deadline that applies in principle (The numbers).
  • D
    The cover letter itself says to read the notes document before arranging land or equipment. It is a reminder not to act on the figures in the response alone.

The wording is from OCCTO Form AP7-20240410, translated here from the Japanese. Dates, receipt numbers and company names are fictitious. Depending on the company, a separate covering letter may be attached, or the cover letter may be worded slightly differently.

What differs between companies is about this: the covering letter, how attachments are added (Tohoku puts the estimated construction cost on a separate sheet), the number of clocks on the construction period (Tokyo has two), the wording (whether "discharge/charge" or "receiving/supply" is used) and additions to the notes. So once you have learned to read one, you can read other companies' responses as well. Where companies vary is the next paper, the interconnection approval.

The six items of the notes document foretell "what happens next"

A "notes document" (Form AP9) comes attached to the response. Its six items are the text of an OCCTO common form, and it is a paper that tends to be skipped. Yet each of the six items says in advance what happens after the response. On each card, the upper part is the gist of the original text and the lower part is how we read it.

Item 1
Not a promise of connection
"By the 'Connection Study Response' … the Company does not promise grid connection"
Connection requires a contract application, and the application requires the "measures required of the applicant" to be in place. The response is an entry paper.
Item 2
Rank at acceptance, seat at approval
"Priority for grid connection is set upon acceptance of the contract application, and … the connection reservation is secured upon interconnection approval"
Holding a response alone does not secure a seat.
Item 3
A desk study; it changes on site
"… is the result of a desk study, and the construction cost contribution and construction period may change as a result of detailed site surveys, land negotiations, outage coordination and the like after the contract application"
"Land negotiations" concerns the land the power company needs to run its lines across.
Item 4
If it changes, a new study
"If the content of the connection study application is changed, or if the facts on which the connection study is premised change, a connection study may be required again"
Which changes count is judged against references such as OCCTO's "Typical examples of minor changes after a contract application." Buyers check that the equipment and output in the seller's documents are the same as the application details in the response.
Item 5
Arrange land and equipment with this understood
"In arranging the land, generation equipment and other items needed for the generation business, please consider the above matters"
A reminder that buying land or ordering equipment on the strength of the figures in the response is done with items 1 to 4 understood. For a buyer, it becomes a question: has the seller paid for land or made advance payments for equipment on the strength of the response alone?
Item 6
Confidentiality
"Use of information learned on the basis of this response for other purposes, disclosure to third parties and the like are prohibited"
A leak may give rise to damages. Each page also carries a line marking it "Confidential." Whether the seller may pass it on unchanged to prospective buyers is a question under this clause.
The one-year validity is written not in the notes document but in section "4. Next procedures" of the attachment. The specimen wording is: "A contract application cannot be accepted in any of the following cases a to c. … c. When one year has passed since the response date of the connection study." The basis is Article 89(1)(vi) of the Transmission and Distribution Business Guidelines. The response date itself is not counted; OCCTO's "The Flow of Grid Access" gives the example that a response dated October 1, 2020 is valid until October 1, 2021. Within that year, the project must get as far as the "acceptance" of its contract application (the day the documents are confirmed complete and payment of the deposit is confirmed).

In the responses we have read, these six items appeared in the notes document of every one of the eight service areas. Kyushu and Kansai also write the validity among the special notes. Tokyo adds the submission of a consent form for non-firm connection; Tohoku adds where to find standard unit prices and that the residual value of removed materials is fixed at settlement. Kyushu also writes that, if the applicant withdraws because land cannot be secured, the deposit is in principle forfeited.

The six places, one line each

How to read it (51-2)JA goes through them one by one in detail; first, one line each.

1

Application details: do the charging kW and equipment match the seller's documents?

Look not only at the discharge output (maximum receiving power) but also at the charging power in parentheses (in many responses, "maximum self-consumption power") and at the type and output of the equipment. If they differ from the documents, they are the seed of a new study, and the value in the response is the older one.

2

Connection feasibility: the proviso next to "yes," and the one sentence on the scope of the study

"Yes" is conditional, with measures as a precondition. The sentence "covering both the reverse flow (receiving) side and the forward flow (supply) side" is a declaration that the study looked not only at discharging but at charging as well. The free pre-consultation does not have this.

3

Outline of the works: is the connection point the nearest line, or a new line from a substation?

The field for the reason for the choice states whether the project goes into the nearest line and, if not the nearest, why (compared on economics; underground because overhead is difficult; and so on). When a new line or works on the upper system are attached, the contribution can change by an order of magnitude, and the construction period changes in years.

4

The contribution breakdown: look at the rows before the total

If there are amounts in the rows for substation equipment, underground lines or upper-system works, the works will take years, and the amount also tends to move at settlement.

5

The starting point of the construction period: not the response date

It is "● years ● months after payment of the construction cost contribution." Add up the contract application, technical review (in principle 6 months), approval, contribution contract and payment, and rebuild the expected connection date yourself.

6

The notes document and the validity: not a promise, may change, confidential, one year

The six cards above, and the validity in section "4. Next procedures" of the body. These are the papers that determine the nature of the contribution figure and of the deadline.

Frequently asked questions

With a connection study response, can a project connect to the grid?

No. The response is the result of a desk study, and the attached notes document states that it does "not promise grid connection." A seat on the grid (connection reservation) gets its place in the queue when the contract application is accepted and is secured with interconnection approval. Holding a response alone does not yet secure a seat.

How long is a connection study response valid?

One year from the day after the response date (Transmission and Distribution Business Guidelines, Article 89(1)(vi)). Within that year the project must get as far as the "acceptance" of its contract application, and acceptance is the day on which the application documents are confirmed complete and payment of the deposit is confirmed. If the year passes, the connection study has to be done again. Kyushu Electric Power Transmission and Distribution also sets the payment deadline for the deposit at this expiry date.

Is the construction period in a response counted from the response date?

No. It is written as "about ● years ● months after payment of the construction cost contribution," and is counted from the date of payment. OCCTO's specimen notes that it does not include the period of the contract procedures (contract application → technical review → interconnection approval → construction cost contribution contract → payment). The technical review takes in principle 6 months from acceptance, and the construction cost contribution contract is due within 1 month of approval (Transmission and Distribution Business Guidelines, Articles 98 and 103). You rebuild the expected connection date yourself by adding these up.
