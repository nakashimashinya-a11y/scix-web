Market Notes

Battery Storage Market Notes, November 2026 issue

A battery storage site earns from three markets: the balancing market (EPRX), wholesale power (JEPX) and the capacity market (OCCTO). Every month we chart the figures for all three from public data and our own records. Charts and tables may be reused with the source stated.

  • In the balancing market (EPRX), Primary reserve (online) went from September 2026 (final) to October 2026 (preliminary) as follows: the bid ratio went from 124.3% to 145.9%, and the average winning price went from 3.42 yen to 3.22 yen (yen/kW per 30 min).
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the average winning price for Primary reserve (offline) went from 5.80 yen in September 2026 (final) to 2.69 yen in October 2026 (preliminary) (yen/kW per 30 min).
  • In wholesale electricity (JEPX), the gap between the average of the most expensive 4 hours and the average of the cheapest 4 hours (monthly average) in October 2026 was 17.14 yen/kWh in Tokyo and 19.30 yen/kWh in Kyushu.

The Barrier

Three barriers for foreign capital

01

Grid access

Connection slots are scarce and slow to secure — and scarcer since the 2026 rules. You cannot simply acquire land and build.

02

Permitting, land & local navigation

Site control, local approvals and grid procedures are Japanese-language, relationship-driven and time-intensive.

03

Market operation

Owning a battery is not revenue. Earning requires bidding and dispatch expertise across the balancing and capacity markets.

Grid-scale battery storage is, at its core, a discipline of measurement and control.

Dr. Shinya Nakashima, ScienceX's Representative Director, began his career in 1990 as a researcher at a pharmaceutical company, working in drug quality control and regulatory submissions to Japan's health ministry. In 1993 he joined Nishikawa Keisoku Co., Ltd. — a specialist trading firm and distributor for Yokogawa Electric and Hewlett-Packard, covering measurement instruments, analytical equipment and plant control systems — where he spent 27 years developing chemical analysis systems and LIMS (laboratory information management systems) and designing GC/MS solutions for residual-pesticide and environmental-contaminant analysis. In all, he spent three decades on the front lines of the technologies that measure, analyze and control.

While still at Nishikawa Keisoku, he pursued doctoral research at the Prefectural University of Kumamoto, earning his PhD in Engineering (environmental and energy engineering) in 2015. His research on carbon-neutral fuels through biomass conversion and torrefaction later became the technical foundation of ScienceX. Alongside academic publications and patents, he served for five years from 2021 as a lecturer in chemistry at Tokyo Kasei University, teaching general and organic chemistry. That classroom experience — making difficult technology clear to non-specialists — carries directly into how he explains battery storage projects today.

In 2021 he founded ScienceX, where he first put his doctoral research to work — developing biogas systems now deployed at three sites across Japan. Today the company's focus is grid-scale battery storage. What determines a battery storage plant's quality and earning power is not the cells alone, but the measurement and control layer — the PCS and EMS. With an eye trained by 30 years in measurement and control, he sources, screens and structures projects — including extra-high-voltage, 50 MW-class installations — with no capital ties to any particular manufacturer or EPC.
