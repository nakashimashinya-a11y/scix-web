When you buy a storage site, you will now need to check the label on its devices. From April 2027, the control devices of grid-connected storage sites need a JC-STAR ★1 label.

So who holds that label? We counted the list published by the Information-technology Promotion Agency, Japan (IPA), one entry at a time. This article is the entry point for those numbers.

Why a storage site needs a label

A storage site does not run on a box of batteries alone. The PCS changes the form of electricity. The BMS watches the battery. The EMS decides when to charge and discharge. Each is a small computer on a network.

Makers and maintenance firms can change settings and fix software from far away. This is convenient. But if someone hijacks that data path, they can stop the site from outside.

Countries have begun to take up this worry. In May 2025, Reuters reported that US experts had found undocumented communication devices in some Chinese-made inverters. The makers and the number of units were not disclosed. In November 2024, Lithuania banned by law remote control from China and other countries over solar, wind and storage plants above 100 kW.

Japan chose to draw the line with a label. For high-voltage and extra-high-voltage solar and batteries whose contract application is accepted on or after 1 April 2027, control devices connected to the internet need ★1. For low voltage, the date is 1 October 2027.

We wrote in 52-3 Existing plants that the starting point is the acceptance date of the contract application. The whole scheme is set out in COLUMN 14.

Terms used in this series

This series uses the terms below. All four articles share the same table.

Table 0 | Terms used in this series (quick reference)
TermMeaning
JC-STARA security labelling scheme for devices connected to the internet (IoT devices). METI launched it in March 2025. The Information-technology Promotion Agency, Japan (IPA) runs it.
★1 (Level 1)The lowest level. The maker checks the basic rules itself and applies. The rules include no default passwords, a contact point for faults, and software that can be fixed. Third-party assessment applies only at ★3 and ★4.
PCS (power conditioning system)The machine that changes the form of electricity between the battery (DC) and the grid (AC). It is the heart of a storage site.
BMSThe battery management system. It watches the temperature and voltage of the battery.
EMSThe energy management system. It decides when to charge and when to discharge.
Remote monitoring / gatewayThe front door that connects a power plant or storage site to outside networks.
The grid / grid connectionThe grid means the transmission and distribution lines of the power companies. Connecting a power plant or storage site to it is called grid connection.
Grid operator (general transmission and distribution operator)The transmission and distribution companies of the large power utilities. There are 10 across Japan, such as Tokyo Electric Power Grid.
Connection studyA procedure in which the grid operator checks whether a site can connect and how much the works will cost. It comes before the contract application.
Contract applicationThe formal application filed after the connection study reply. It counts as accepted only when the documents are complete and the deposit has been paid.
Extra-high voltage, high voltage, low voltageThe voltage classes of the connection. High voltage is roughly under 2,000 kW and low voltage is under 50 kW. Extra-high voltage is larger than both.

How we chose the 189: we selected battery systems, PCS, BMS, EMS, metering and control units and remote monitoring from the product names and the description on each product page. General-purpose routers and cameras are not included.

★1 is the lowest level. The maker checks that it meets the rules and applies to IPA. No product has yet been assessed by a third party at ★3 or ★4.

So today, "★1 obtained" means this: the maker has checked that the basic locks are in place, and IPA has accepted the application.

Which countries' companies hold labels

Figure 1 splits the 189 solar and battery entries by the country of each company's headquarters. A Japanese subsidiary of a foreign parent is counted under the parent's country.

Japan has 137, or 70% of the total. In the grid-scale group these include Fuji Electric, Nissin Electric, TMEIC, Meidensha, Daihen, GS Yuasa, Sumitomo Electric, NGK Insulators and PowerX. In the residential group they include Nichicon, Choshu Sangyo, Panasonic, Kyocera and Omron.

Only one entry comes from a company headquartered in China. It is a residential metering and control unit from Suntech Power Japan.

The 11 entries marked "HQ not confirmed" include 3 RENON POWER batteries. The company says its headquarters is in the US, but it also has affiliates in China, and we could not confirm the parent's country.

The big Chinese makers are not listed yet

On 1 July 2026, Nikkei reported that the products of Chinese makers, which hold a large share of Japan's PCS and battery market, were not on the list. The 12 November list is the same. The table below shows where the 16 big Chinese-headquartered makers stand now.

IPA's list shows only labels that have been issued. From outside, we cannot tell whether a company has applied and is waiting, or has not applied.

Why have they not obtained labels? An executive of a Chinese maker told Nikkei: "Only Chinese companies' applications are being rejected. It is a de facto exclusion." METI said it judges "comprehensively, on the basis of the submitted information", and does not disclose individual decisions.

According to Nikkei, the application checklist has an item asking whether the company could be affected by foreign laws. Nikkei also reports an expert view that China's National Intelligence Law collides with this item. But the real reason cannot be confirmed from outside.

GoodWe's plan is shaped to fit the line of the requirement. ANRE's FAQ excludes devices without IP communication. But a device with IP communication needs ★1 even if that function is left unused. Whether the plan will be accepted has to be checked with the grid operator, project by project.

Labels were obtained in three waves

Figure 2 shows the number of labels obtained each month. There are three peaks.

  1. October 2025 (24). Domestic grid-scale companies such as Fuji Electric, Nissin Electric, Daihen and Shirokuma Denryoku moved first. This was before the 20th Grid Code Review Committee (16 December 2025), which set out the direction of the requirement.
  2. March to April 2026 (48). Mostly residential: 30 of the 48.
  3. From June 2026. Grid-scale products from Taiwan and Korea came in.

The first expiries come in May 2027

A ★1 label is valid for up to 2 years from the day it was obtained. The 10 labels obtained in May 2025 expire between 6 and 27 May 2027. That is the month after the requirement starts.

IPA said it would announce the details of the extension procedure "in 2026". As of 15 November, it has not done so. We list which products expire and when in 52-2 The list.

What a buyer checks is the registration number

The wording of Japan's requirement has no nationality condition. Even so, the big Chinese names do not appear on the list. The reason cannot be known from outside.

A buyer can check two things. Does the model number of the project's device match a product on IPA's list? And is that registration number valid now? The table for checking is in the next article, 52-2 The list.

Frequently asked questions

How many JC-STAR certified products are there now?

IPA's list of products with the conformity label has 395 entries, as updated on 12 November 2026. All are ★1, and none is ★2 or above. By our count, 189 of them relate to solar and batteries (battery systems, PCS, BMS, EMS, metering and control units, and remote monitoring).

Do Chinese PCS and battery makers hold JC-STAR labels?

On the 12 November list, the 16 big Chinese makers, including Sungrow, Huawei, CATL, BYD and GoodWe, hold none under their own names. The only Chinese-headquartered company on the list is Suntech Power Japan, with one residential metering and control unit. The Chinese makers say their applications have been rejected, and METI does not disclose individual decisions (Nikkei, 1 July 2026).

Why does a storage site need JC-STAR?

The PCS, BMS, EMS and remote monitoring units are small computers on a network. If someone hijacks the data path, they can stop the site from outside. So for high-voltage and extra-high-voltage solar and battery projects whose contract application is accepted on or after 1 April 2027, devices with IP communication need ★1. For low voltage, the date is 1 October 2027.
