Battery Storage Market Notes, July 2026 issue

The price of electricity changes with the time of day it is used. A "grid-scale battery" (a large battery connected to the power grid that stores electricity and releases it) earns money from that difference. These notes use published figures and our own records to show this month's trends in figures and tables. This issue is a back issue, produced retroactively in November 2026. The grid connection study figures are as of the date it was produced (October 19, 2026).

01

The three income streams of a battery storage plant (how to read these notes)

A battery storage plant (grid-scale battery) earns its income from three markets. ① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) is the market where you buy electricity when it is cheap, store it, and sell it when it is expensive. ② The balancing market (EPRX) is the market where you sell grid operators the "capacity" to correct surpluses or shortages of electricity. ③ The capacity market (OCCTO) is the market where the country buys future electricity supply capacity in advance. These notes present the figures in the order of these three markets. The ①②③ at the start of each section title shows which market the section covers.

Source: Rules of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX) and the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO).

02

Three things that happened this month

We picked three trends from the figures of the three markets this month.

  • In wholesale electricity (JEPX), the price gap between the average of the 4 most expensive hours and the 4 cheapest hours, averaged over July 2026, was 13.17 yen/kWh in Tokyo and 15.71 yen/kWh in Kyushu.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the bid ratio for Primary reserve (online) first went above 100% since April 2025 in June 2026 (106.8%). This month it was 119.7%, and the average winning price was 4.35 yen/kW per 30 min.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), 57.3% of the month's sales of Tertiary reserve 2 (average winning price × winning volume) came from the top 10% of 30-minute slots.
03

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | Average price

Wholesale electricity is electricity bought and sold among power companies and retailers. Its price is set on the "spot market" of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), a market where electricity for the next day is traded the day before. "System (nationwide)" is the price when all of Japan is treated as one market (the system price). When the power lines between regions get congested, separate prices form for each area from Hokkaido to Kyushu (area prices).

The monthly average of wholesale electricity for System (nationwide) was 17.91 yen/kWh in July 2026, against 12.75 yen/kWh in the same month last year.

Source: Spot market trading results published by the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX). Simple average of the 30-minute prices. Our tally.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How the price moves through the day

The wholesale price changes every 30 minutes. The figure shows the shape of a typical day, with prices at the same time of day averaged over the month. If a battery charges when power is cheap and discharges when it is expensive, the difference becomes its income.

The daily low of wholesale electricity is 15.28 yen around 6:00 in Tokyo and 9.85 yen around 12:30 in Kyushu. The peak is around 18:30 in both areas: 28.07 yen in Tokyo and 27.02 yen in Kyushu (yen/kWh).

Source: JEPX spot market. Our tally.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How big is the price gap per kWh

For each area, we took the average price of the day's 4 most expensive hours minus the average price of its 4 cheapest hours, and show the monthly average. This equals the difference per kWh when you buy 1 kWh in the 4 cheapest hours and sell it in the 4 most expensive hours. It is a simple gap. It does not deduct electricity lost in charging and discharging or fees, and it ignores whether the cheap hours come before the expensive hours.

By area, the monthly average gap between the 4 most expensive and the 4 cheapest hours of wholesale electricity was largest in Shikoku at 16.78 yen/kWh and smallest in Tokyo at 13.17 yen/kWh.

07

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Seven products, and prices by area

The balancing market is where grid operators buy the "capacity" to correct surpluses or shortages of electricity, every 30 minutes. Products are divided by how quickly output changes after an instruction. Primary reserve is the fastest and Tertiary reserve 2 is the slowest. Primary reserve has two categories. "Online" is connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line. "Offline" runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line. They are tendered and traded separately, so we show them separately. A Composite product is a bid that bundles several products. The price is set in yen per 1 kW of available capacity per 30 minutes. The figure shows the range of winning prices by area (by the area where the power source is). The thin line runs from the cheapest to the most expensive winning bid in the month. The thick band is the price range that holds 8 out of every 10 30-minute slots with winning bids. The dot is the average winning price. Nationwide figures are in the table in the "Market figures" section.

In the balancing market, the highest average winning price by area is Primary reserve (offline) in Tohoku at 14.49 yen. The lowest is Tertiary reserve 2 in Shikoku at 0.38 yen (yen/kW per 30 min).

Source: Trading results (preliminary figures) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our tally.

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Grid areas and times where bids win more easily

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Primary reserve (online) turned into a contest for seats

Primary reserve is the fastest product. It changes output within 10 seconds of an instruction, and batteries are often used for it. The left shows the online category and the right the offline category. The bars are the bid ratio (bid volume ÷ volume tendered by the grid operators). Above 100%, more bids come in than are tendered, and some bids lose. The line is the average winning price. These are the monthly figures we have, starting from April 2025. Figures for Primary reserve (offline) start in April 2026.

In the balancing market, the bid ratio for Primary reserve (online) went above 100% in June 2026. This month it is 119.7%, with an average winning price of 4.35 yen. Primary reserve (offline) is at 65.1% and 10.25 yen (yen/kW per 30 min).

Source: Trading results (preliminary figures) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our tally.

③ Capacity market (OCCTO) | A market that buys future supply capacity

The capacity market buys electricity supply capacity 4 years ahead in a once-a-year auction. The price is set in yen per kW per year. The left figure shows clearing prices in past main auctions. The right figure shows the battery and pumped-hydro capacity that cleared in the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction (a separate auction that supports new builds such as batteries and pumped hydro). Results are published once a year, so they do not change every month. The latest is for delivery FY2029 (published January 2026).

In the capacity market (once a year, latest publication), the clearing price in Tokyo was 3,495 yen/kW for delivery FY2025 and 15,111 yen/kW for FY2029. In the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, batteries and pumped hydro cleared 819 MW of lithium-ion and 886 MW of non-lithium in the FY2025 round.

Source: Clearing results and capacity securing contract documents published by the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) (sources listed in the last section (Assumptions and terms)). As published.

Our records | How many months and how much it takes to connect to the grid

A grid connection study is the procedure where the grid operator checks "can this site connect, and how much will the work cost?" The grid connection cost contribution is the part of the connection work cost paid by the applicant. Of the response letters we received, we tallied only high-voltage cases (connecting to 6,600 V distribution lines), by grid area. Extra-high-voltage cases are not included. Grid areas with fewer than 5 cases are not shown.

Grid connection study responses (high voltage only, as of October 19, 2026): the median construction period ranges from 8.0 months in Tokyo to 17.0 months in Tohoku. The median grid connection cost contribution ranges from ¥3.02 million in Tokyo to ¥32.35 million in Tohoku.

Source: Our tally of the high-voltage grid connection study response letters we received from general transmission and distribution operators (as of October 19, 2026).

Assumptions and terms

  • The figures are our tally of the published figures in the sources. We have not added up several months or converted anything to annual figures. We do not show the earnings of any single battery storage plant.
  • Months covered: wholesale electricity (JEPX) covers July 2026 (31 days). The balancing market (EPRX) covers the July 2026 preliminary figures (delivery dates up to the 31st). For the balancing market, the month differs until the published figures for July 2026 are included in the tally.
  • Average prices are simple averages of 30-minute slots (not weighted by trading volume). Daytime means the 8:00–16:00 slots and evening means the 16:00–20:00 slots.
  • Price gap: we rank the day's 30-minute slots by price and subtract the average of the 8 cheapest slots from the average of the 8 most expensive slots (4 hours) (4 slots for 2 hours). It ignores the order of the cheap and expensive hours, electricity lost in charging and discharging, and fees, taxes and grid usage charges. It does not show actual earnings.
  • Balancing market: nationwide figures (total column) of the EPRX preliminary figures. Tendered volume is by grid operator (TSO). Bid volume, winning volume and average winning price are by area where the power source is. Volumes are averages per 30-minute slot. The average winning price is weighted by winning volume. Bid ratio = bid volume ÷ tendered volume; clearing rate = winning volume ÷ bid volume. The 3-hour blocks up to March 13, 2026 were counted as six 30-minute slots per block.
  • Range of winning prices: the thin line runs from the cheapest bid (lowest winning price) to the most expensive bid (highest winning price) among the month's 30-minute slots with winning bids (published figures by area where the power source is). The published figures for Primary reserve (online), Secondary reserve 1 and 2 and Tertiary reserve 1 also include Composite product bids (one price for several bundled products). For this reason, we removed from the thin line any bids that can be explained by a Composite product bid in the same 30-minute slot and area. These are bids above the price cap of 15 yen (for deliveries up to August 31, 2026) for Primary reserve (online) and Secondary reserve 1, and bids above 10 yen for Secondary reserve 2 and Tertiary reserve 1. For Primary reserve (offline), we removed bids above the price cap (15 yen this month) from the thin line (the balancing market system also clears bids above the cap, then cuts them down to the cap at settlement). The thick band covers the 10% to 90% range when the average winning prices of slots with winning bids are ranked from lowest to highest. The dot is the average weighted by winning volume.
  • Clearing rate by grid area × time of day: total winning volume ÷ total bid volume for the slots at the same time of day in the month (by area where the power source is).
  • The time from instruction to output change and the duration for each balancing market product are the FY2026 specifications. The online category of Primary reserve is connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line. The offline category runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line.
  • High-price 30-minute slots for Tertiary reserve 2: slots with a nationwide average winning price of 10 yen/kW per 30 min or more. The top 1% means the top 1% of slots when ranked by sales (average winning price × winning volume). The highest winning price is the published figure by area where the power source is.
