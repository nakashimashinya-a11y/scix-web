Battery Storage Market Notes, May 2026 issue

The price of electricity changes with the time of day. Grid-scale battery storage (large batteries connected to the grid that store electricity and release it) earns money from that difference. These notes use published figures and our own records to show this month's trends in figures and tables. This issue is a back issue, produced in November 2026 for an earlier month. The grid connection study figures are as of the date we produced it (October 19, 2026).

01

The 3 income sources of a battery storage plant (how to read these notes)

A battery storage plant (grid-scale battery storage) earns income from 3 markets. ① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) is the market where you buy and store electricity when it is cheap and sell it when it is expensive. ② The balancing market (EPRX) is the market where you sell grid operators the "capacity" to correct surpluses and shortages of electricity. ③ The capacity market (OCCTO) is the market where the country buys future electricity supply capacity in advance. These notes present the figures in the order of these 3 markets. The ①, ② or ③ at the start of each section title shows which market it covers.

Source: Rules of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX) and the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO).

02

3 trends this month

From the figures for the 3 markets, we picked 3 trends this month.

  • In wholesale electricity (JEPX), the monthly average spread between the average of the 4 highest-priced hours and the average of the 4 lowest-priced hours in May 2026 was 18.00 yen/kWh in Tokyo and 16.64 yen/kWh in Kyushu.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the bid ratio for Primary reserve (online) this month was 71.0%. No month since April 2025 has yet exceeded 100%. The average awarded price was 4.84 yen/kW per 30 min.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the top 10% of 30-minute slots accounted for 54.2% of the month's revenue for Tertiary reserve 2.
03

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | Average price

Wholesale electricity is electricity that power companies and retailers buy and sell among themselves. Its price is set on the "spot market" of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), a market where electricity for the next day is traded the day before. "System (nationwide)" is the price when all of Japan is treated as a single market (the system price). When the power lines between regions are congested, the price splits into a separate price for each area from Hokkaido to Kyushu (the area price).

The monthly average wholesale price for System (nationwide) was 14.06 yen/kWh in May 2026, against 8.92 yen/kWh in the same month last year (a difference of 5.14 yen).

Source: Spot market trading results published by the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX). Simple average of 30-minute prices. Our own aggregation.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How the price moves through the day

The wholesale electricity price changes every 30 minutes. The figure shows the shape of one day, averaging the prices at the same time of day across the month. If a battery stores electricity when it is cheap and releases it when it is expensive, the difference becomes the battery's income.

The daily low and high of wholesale electricity were 9.96 yen/kWh around 7:00 and 28.79 yen/kWh around 18:30 in Tokyo, and 1.46 yen/kWh around 12:00 and 19.98 yen/kWh around 18:30 in Kyushu.

Source: JEPX spot market. Our own aggregation.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How large is the spread per kWh

For each area, we took the average price of the day's 4 highest-priced hours, subtracted the average price of the 4 lowest-priced hours, and show the monthly average. This equals the difference per kWh if you buy 1 kWh in the 4 cheapest hours and sell it in the 4 most expensive hours. It is a simple difference: it does not subtract electricity lost in charging and discharging or fees, and it ignores the order of the cheap and expensive hours.

By area, the monthly average 4-hour wholesale spread was largest in Tohoku at 21.14 yen/kWh and smallest in Shikoku at 12.97 yen/kWh.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | There is a spread every day

We show the same 4-hour spread day by day. The larger the spread on a given day, the more room a battery has to earn from it.

07

② Balancing market (EPRX) | 7 products, and prices by area

The balancing market is a market where grid operators buy, every 30 minutes, the "capacity" to correct surpluses and shortages of electricity. Products differ by how quickly output must change after an instruction. Primary reserve is the fastest and Tertiary reserve 2 is the slowest. Primary reserve has 2 categories: "online", connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line, and "offline", which runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line. They are tendered and traded separately, so we show them separately. A Composite product is a bid that offers several products together. The price is set in yen per 1 kW of capacity offered per 30 minutes. The figure shows the range of awarded prices by area (by the area where the resource is located). The thin line runs from the lowest to the highest awarded bid in the month, the thick band is the price range that contains 8 out of 10 of the 30-minute slots with awards, and the dot is the average awarded price. Nationwide figures are in the table in the section "Market figures".

By area, the highest average awarded price in the balancing market was 13.00 yen/kW per 30 min for Tertiary reserve 2 in Tokyo, and the lowest was 0.33 yen/kW per 30 min, also for Tertiary reserve 2, in Shikoku.

Source: Trading results (preliminary figures) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our own aggregation.

09

② Balancing market (EPRX) | How full are the seats for Primary reserve

Primary reserve is the fastest product, changing output within 10 seconds of an instruction, and batteries are often used for it. The left side is the online category and the right side is the offline category. The bars show the bid ratio (volume offered ÷ volume requested by grid operators). Above 100%, more bids come in than the volume requested, and some bids lose. The line shows the average awarded price. These are monthly figures from April 2025, the data we have. Primary reserve (offline) figures start in April 2026.

In the balancing market, the bid ratio and average awarded price (yen/kW per 30 min) were 71.0% and 4.84 yen for Primary reserve (online), with no month above 100% yet, and 40.4% and 12.53 yen for Primary reserve (offline).

Source: Trading results (preliminary figures) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our own aggregation.

14

③ Capacity market (OCCTO) | A market that buys future supply capacity

The capacity market buys electricity supply capacity 4 years ahead through an annual auction. The price is set in yen per kW per year. The left figure shows the clearing prices of past main auctions. The right figure shows the battery and pumped hydro capacity contracted in the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction (a separate auction that supports new builds such as batteries and pumped hydro). These are published once a year, so they do not change every month. The latest is delivery FY2029 (published January 2026).

In the capacity market, Tokyo's clearing price was 14,137 yen/kW for delivery FY2024, 3,495 yen/kW for FY2025, 5,834 yen/kW for FY2026 and 15,111 yen/kW for FY2029. In the capacity market's Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, contracted battery and pumped hydro capacity in the FY2025 auction was 819 MW for lithium-ion and 886 MW for non-lithium. This is the latest annual publication, so it does not change every month.

Source: Auction results and capacity contract documents published by the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) (sources in the last section (Assumptions and terms)). As published.

Our records | How many months and how much it costs to connect to the grid

A grid connection study is the procedure where the grid operator checks "whether you can connect at this location, and how much the work will cost". The grid connection cost contribution is the part of the connection work paid by the applicant. Of the reply documents we received, we aggregated only the high-voltage ones (connecting to 6,600V distribution lines) by grid area. Extra-high voltage is not included. Grid areas with fewer than 5 cases are not shown.

Medians of the grid connection study replies (high voltage only, as of October 19, 2026) ranged from 8.0 months in Tokyo to 17.0 months in Tohoku for the construction period, and from ¥3.02 million in Tokyo to ¥32.35 million in Tohoku for the grid connection cost contribution.

Source: Aggregation of high-voltage grid connection study replies from general transmission and distribution operators received by us (as of October 19, 2026).

Assumptions and terms

  • The figures are our own aggregation of the published figures in the sources. We have not added up several months or converted figures to annual values. We do not show revenue amounts for any single battery storage plant.
  • Months covered: wholesale electricity (JEPX) is May 2026 (31 days), and the balancing market (EPRX) is May 2026 preliminary figures (delivery dates up to the 31st). For the balancing market, the month differs until the published figures for May 2026 are included in our aggregation.
  • Average prices are simple averages of 30-minute slots (not weighted by trading volume). Daytime is the slots from 8:00 to 16:00, and evening is the slots from 16:00 to 20:00.
  • Spread: the day's 30-minute slots are ranked by price, and the average of the 8 lowest slots is subtracted from the average of the 8 highest slots (4 hours) (4 slots for 2 hours). This ignores the order of the cheap and expensive hours, electricity lost in charging and discharging, and fees, taxes and grid usage charges. It does not represent actual revenue.
  • Balancing market: nationwide figures (total column) from EPRX preliminary figures. Requested volume is by grid operator (TSO); offered volume, awarded volume and average awarded price are by the area where the resource is located. Volumes are averages per 30-minute slot. The average awarded price is weighted by awarded volume. Bid ratio = volume offered ÷ volume requested; award rate = volume awarded ÷ volume offered. For 3-hour blocks up to March 13, 2026, we counted each block as 6 30-minute slots.
  • Range of awarded prices: the thin line runs, across the month's 30-minute slots with awards, from the lowest bid (minimum awarded price) to the highest bid (maximum awarded price) (published figures by the area where the resource is located). The published figures for Primary reserve (online), Secondary reserve 1 and 2 and Tertiary reserve 1 also include Composite product bids (a single price for several products combined). So we removed from the thin line bids above the price cap of 15 yen for Primary reserve (online) and Secondary reserve 1 (for delivery up to August 31, 2026), and above 10 yen for Secondary reserve 2 and Tertiary reserve 1, where a Composite product bid in the same 30-minute slot and area can explain them. For Primary reserve (offline), we removed from the thin line bids above the price cap (15 yen this month) (the balancing market system also awards bids above the cap, and cuts them down to the cap at settlement). The thick band is the range from the 10% to the 90% point when the average awarded prices of the slots with awards are ranked from lowest to highest. The dot is the average weighted by awarded volume.
  • Award rate by grid area × time of day: total awarded volume ÷ total offered volume for the slots at the same time of day across the month (by the area where the resource is located).
  • The time from instruction to output change and the duration for balancing market products are the FY2026 specifications. For Primary reserve, the online category connects to the grid operator by a dedicated line, and the offline category runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line.
  • High-priced slots for Tertiary reserve 2: slots with a nationwide average awarded price of 10 yen/kW per 30 min or more. The top 1% is the top 1% of slots ranked by revenue (average awarded price × awarded volume). The maximum awarded price is the published figure by the area where the resource is located.
