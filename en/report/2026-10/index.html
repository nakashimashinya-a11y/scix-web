Grid-Scale Battery Storage Monthly Report, October 2026

The highest income estimates for October were Chubu at ¥10.43 million, Hokkaido at ¥8.07 million and Kyushu at ¥7.45 million. The lowest was Tokyo at ¥5.30 million.

The price of electricity changes with the time of day it is used. A "grid-scale battery" (a large battery connected to the power grid that stores electricity and releases it) earns money from that price difference. This monthly report uses published figures and our own records to show this month's trends in charts.

We estimated, for each of the 9 grid areas, how much income one battery site with 2 MW output and 8 MWh capacity would have earned in October 2026. The income estimate is the capacity market amount plus the larger of electricity trading (price spread) and the balancing market. The price spread and the balancing market compete for the same 2 MW, so we do not add both. It is an income estimate before costs such as aggregator fees, maintenance and taxes. It does not show or guarantee actual earnings. The balancing market uses September figures (September 1–29). It assumes the site won in every 30-minute slot that had winning bids, so it comes out higher than reality (section 6). The capacity market amount is the clearing price of the latest auction that a battery site built today would enter (FY2029), divided by 12 months.

02

Three trends this month

Wholesale electricity is electricity bought and sold by power companies and retailers. Its price is set on the "spot market" of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX) (a market where electricity for the next day is traded the day before).

  • The average wholesale electricity price in October was 27.02 yen/kWh in Tokyo (same month last year 13.06 yen/kWh, a difference of 13.96 yen) and 15.07 yen/kWh in Kyushu (same month last year 10.74 yen/kWh, a difference of 4.34 yen).
  • Within the day, the cheapest average was around 12:00 in both Tokyo and Kyushu (Tokyo 19.03 yen, Kyushu 4.94 yen). The highest was 37.11 yen around 16:30 in Tokyo and 24.74 yen around 17:30 in Kyushu.
  • In our estimate for one battery site in Tokyo, the monthly figures are ¥2.78 million from the electricity price spread (October), ¥1.31 million from Tertiary reserve 2 in the balancing market (September) and ¥2.52 million from the capacity market (FY2029 price).

Source: Spot market trading results published by the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX). Simple average of prices for each 30 minutes. Our tally.

03

How the price of electricity moves within a day

The price of electricity changes every 30 minutes. The chart shows the shape of a day, averaging prices at the same time across the month. If a battery stores electricity when it is cheap and releases it when it is expensive, the difference becomes the battery's income.

The time-of-day averages ranged from 19.03 yen around 12:00 to 37.11 yen around 16:30 in Tokyo, and from 4.94 yen around 12:00 to 24.74 yen around 17:30 in Kyushu.

Source: JEPX spot market. Our tally.

04

There is a high-low gap every day

We lined up, day by day, the difference between the highest and lowest price within each day. The larger the gap on a day, the more room a battery has to earn from the price spread.

The monthly average of the daily high-low gap was 36.50 yen in Hokkaido, 24.05 yen in Kansai, 23.15 yen in Kyushu and 21.87 yen in Tokyo. Slots priced at 0.01 yen occurred 91 times in Kyushu and 16 times in Tokyo.

Source: JEPX spot market. Our tally.

05

Three income sources for a battery site

A battery site has three main sources of income. The electricity price spread (wholesale power market), the balancing market (a market for buying and selling the "ability" to fix surpluses or shortages of electricity), and the capacity market (a market that buys future electricity supply capacity in advance). In the capacity market, power plants and battery sites receive a fixed income per kW.

Source: Rules of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX) and the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO).

How much does a battery earn? (estimate)

These are monthly figures for one battery site with 2 MW output and 8 MWh capacity. For electricity trading, we assume it charges in the cheapest 4 hours of the day and discharges in the most expensive 4 hours. For the balancing market, we assume it keeps offering 2 MW in Tertiary reserve 2. For the capacity market, the amount is the clearing price × 2,000 kW ÷ 12. All are rough estimates. They do not show or guarantee actual earnings (assumptions in section 12). The gold bars for the balancing market are September figures (September 1–29), a different month from the navy bars for October.

In our estimate for one battery site, Tokyo had ¥2.78 million from the price spread (October), ¥1.31 million from Tertiary reserve 2 (September) and ¥2.52 million from the capacity market (per month). Kyushu had ¥3.68 million, ¥4.93 million and ¥2.52 million respectively.

Source: Our estimate based on the JEPX spot market, EPRX preliminary figures and clearing prices published by OCCTO. Assumptions in section 12.

Batteries are also used in the balancing market

In the balancing market, the lower the product number, the faster the response required. The chart shows the share of batteries in the volume awarded. Published figures for October 2026 are not yet in our tally, so we show September 2026 (preliminary), the latest month with all products available.

In September 2026 (preliminary), the battery share of awarded volume was 27.9% for Primary reserve, 10.4% for Composite product, 1.8% for Secondary reserve 1 and 0.8% for Tertiary reserve 2.

Source: Trading results by power source type (preliminary) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our tally.

08

The capacity market buys future supply capacity

The capacity market buys electricity supply capacity four years ahead through an auction once a year. The price is set in yen per kW per year. The left chart shows past main auction clearing prices. The right chart shows the battery and pumped hydro capacity contracted in the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction (a separate auction that supports new builds such as batteries and pumped hydro). Results are published once a year, so they do not change every month. The latest is FY2029 (published January 2026).

These are the latest figures, published once a year. In the capacity market, Tokyo was 3,495 yen/kW for FY2025 and 15,111 yen/kW for FY2029. In the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, batteries and pumped hydro in the FY2025 auction were 819 MW lithium-ion and 886 MW non-lithium.

Source: Auction results and capacity contract documents published by the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) (sources in section 12). As published.

How many months and how much does it take to connect to the grid?

A grid connection study is a procedure where the grid operator checks "can this site connect, and how much will the work cost?" The grid connection cost contribution is the part of the connection work cost paid by the applicant. From the responses we received, we tallied only high-voltage cases (connecting to 6,600 V distribution lines) by grid area. Extra-high voltage is not included. Grid areas with fewer than 5 cases are not shown.

As of October 19, 2026, the high-voltage medians were 8.0 months and ¥3.02 million in Tokyo, 12.5 months and ¥12.50 million in Kyushu, and 17.0 months and ¥32.35 million in Tohoku.

Source: Our tally of high-voltage grid connection study responses received from grid operators (as of October 19, 2026).

12

Estimate assumptions and terms

  • The estimates are rough figures we calculated from published prices and the assumptions below. They do not show or guarantee actual earnings.
  • Battery site: 2 MW output, 8 MWh capacity, round-trip efficiency 85%, depth of discharge 95%. Maximum 2 MW for both charging and discharging.
  • Electricity trading: of the day's 30-minute slots, it charges in the 8 cheapest slots and discharges in the 8 most expensive. The charge amount is capacity × depth of discharge, and the discharge amount is 85% of that. We do not account for slot order, state-of-charge limits, the effect of our own trades on the market, or fees, taxes and grid costs.
  • Balancing market: the total if the site offered 2 MW in Tertiary reserve 2 in every slot and won at the average winning price (yen/kW per 30 min) of the area where the power source is located in that slot. Slots with no winning bids count as 0. In reality, there are slots where the site would lose to competition, and offering reserve leaves times when the battery cannot be used for trading, so this assumption comes out higher than reality.
  • Capacity market: the latest main auction (FY2029) clearing price (area price, yen/kW per year) × rated output 2000 kW ÷ 12. The income is received in that delivery fiscal year. It does not account for the coefficient used to rate supply capacity, deductions under transitional measures, or reductions from effectiveness tests or assessments. Income from the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction is not included.
  • Months covered: electricity trading uses October (31 days), the balancing market uses September (September 1–29). The months differ until the October 2026 balancing market figures are included in our tally.
  • Figures are shown as they appear in the sources. We do not add up multiple months or convert to annual figures (the monthly capacity market amount is the annual clearing price divided by 12).
  • Average prices are simple averages of 30-minute slots (not weighted by trading volume). Daytime means slots from 8:00 to 16:00, evening means slots from 16:00 to 20:00.
