Battery Storage Market Notes, October 2026 issue

The price of electricity changes with the time of day it is used. A "grid-scale battery" (a large battery connected to the power grid that stores electricity and charges and discharges it) earns money from that difference. These notes use published figures and our own records to show this month's developments in figures and tables.

01

Three revenue streams for a battery storage site (how to read these notes)

A battery storage site (grid-scale battery) earns its income from three markets. ① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) is the market where you buy and store electricity when it is cheap and sell it when it is expensive. ② The balancing market (EPRX) is the market where you sell grid operators the "capability" to fix surpluses and shortages of electricity. ③ The capacity market (OCCTO) is the market where the country buys future electricity supply capacity in advance. These notes present figures in the order of these three markets. The ①②③ at the start of each section title shows which market it covers.

Source: Rules of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX) and the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO).

02

Three developments this month

We picked three developments this month from the figures of the three markets.

  • In wholesale electricity (JEPX), the monthly average spread between the 4 most expensive hours and the 4 cheapest hours in October 2026 was 17.14 yen/kWh in Tokyo and 19.30 yen/kWh in Kyushu.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the bid-to-requirement ratio of Primary reserve (online) exceeded 100% for the first time in June 2026, at 106.8%. In September it was 123.1%, and the average winning price was 3.43 yen/kW per 30 min.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), 56.9% of September 2026 revenue for Tertiary reserve 2 came from the top 10% of 30-minute slots.
03

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | Average prices

Wholesale electricity is the electricity that power companies and retailers buy and sell. Its price is set on the "spot market" of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), where electricity for the next day is traded the day before. "System (nationwide)" is the price when all of Japan is treated as one market (the system price). When the transmission lines between regions are congested, the price splits by area, from Hokkaido to Kyushu (the area price).

The October 2026 monthly average for wholesale electricity was 22.51 yen/kWh for System (nationwide), against 11.67 yen/kWh in the same month last year, a difference of 10.84 yen.

Source: Spot market trading results published by the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX). Simple average of the price for each 30 minutes. Our compilation.

04

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How the price moves through the day

The wholesale electricity price changes every 30 minutes. The figure shows the shape of an average day, averaging the price at the same time of day across the month. If a battery stores electricity in cheap hours and releases it in expensive hours, the difference becomes its income.

The daily low and high of wholesale electricity were 19.03 yen around 12:00 and 37.11 yen around 16:30 in Tokyo, and 4.94 yen around 12:00 and 24.74 yen around 17:30 in Kyushu.

Source: JEPX spot market. Our compilation.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How large is the spread per kWh

For each area, we took the average price of the day's 4 most expensive hours minus the average price of its 4 cheapest hours, and show the monthly average. This equals the difference per kWh if you buy 1 kWh in the cheap 4 hours and sell it in the expensive 4 hours. It is a simple difference. It does not subtract the electricity lost in charging and discharging or fees, and it ignores whether the cheap hours come before the expensive hours.

The monthly average spread between the expensive 4 hours and the cheap 4 hours of wholesale electricity was largest in Hokkaido at 29.02 yen/kWh and smallest in Tokyo at 17.14 yen/kWh.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | There is a spread every day

We show the same 4-hour spread day by day. The larger the spread on a given day, the more room a battery has to earn from it.

The 4-hour spread of wholesale electricity was 10 yen or more on 29 of 31 days for System (nationwide), on 31 days in Hokkaido, Hokuriku, Kansai, Chugoku and Kyushu, and on 28 days in Shikoku.

07

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Seven products and prices by area

The balancing market is where grid operators buy, every 30 minutes, the "capability" to fix surpluses and shortages of electricity. Products are divided by how fast output must change after a dispatch instruction. Primary reserve is the fastest and Tertiary reserve 2 is the slowest. Primary reserve has two categories: "online", connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line, and "offline", operated through the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line. They are tendered and traded separately, so we show them separately. A Composite product is a bid that offers several products together. Prices are set in yen per 1 kW of available capability per 30 minutes. The figure shows the range of winning prices by area (by the area where the power source is located). The thin line runs from the cheapest to the most expensive winning bid in the month. The thick band is the price range that contains 8 out of 10 of the 30-minute slots with winning bids. The dot is the average winning price. Nationwide figures are in the table in the "Market figures" section. Published figures for October 2026 are not yet in our compilation, so we show the most recent month with all products available, September 2026 (days 1–29) (preliminary figures).

In September, the average winning price by area in the balancing market was highest for Primary reserve (offline) in Tohoku at 9.51 yen and lowest for Tertiary reserve 2 in Shikoku at 0.38 yen (yen/kW per 30 min).

Source: Trading results (preliminary) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our compilation.

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Grid areas and times where bids win more easily

The clearing rate is the share of bid volume that wins. The higher it is, the more likely a submitted bid is to win. The figure plots, for each grid area (the area where the power source is located), the clearing rate of each product by time of day (every 30 minutes). Where a line is broken, there were no bids at that time.

Source: Trading results (preliminary) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our compilation.

09

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Primary reserve (online) became a competition for seats

Primary reserve is the fastest product. It changes output within 10 seconds of an instruction, and batteries are often used for it. The left side shows the online category and the right side the offline category. The bars are the bid-to-requirement ratio (volume bid ÷ volume the grid operators tendered). Above 100%, more bids come in than the tendered volume, and some bids lose. The line is the average winning price. These are the monthly figures we have, starting in April 2025. Figures for Primary reserve (offline) start in April 2026.

In the balancing market, the bid-to-requirement ratio of Primary reserve (online) exceeded 100% in June 2026. In September it was 123.1%, with an average winning price of 3.43 yen. Primary reserve (offline) was at 84.0% and 5.79 yen.

Source: Trading results (preliminary) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our compilation.

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Revenue is concentrated in a small number of 30-minute slots

We ranked the month's 30-minute slots with winning bids by revenue (average winning price × winning volume, nationwide), from highest to lowest. For each product, we plotted what % of the month's revenue the top slots account for. The more a curve bulges above the dotted line, the more revenue is concentrated in a few slots. Tertiary reserve 2 is a product that covers errors in forecasting renewable output caused by weather. Its price is low in most slots and high in a small number of slots.

In September, the share of balancing market revenue concentrated in the top 10% of slots was 56.9% for Tertiary reserve 2 and 15.8% for Primary reserve (online) (10.1% and 2.1% for the top 1%).

14

③ Capacity market (OCCTO) | A market that buys future supply capacity

The capacity market buys electricity supply capacity 4 years ahead through an auction held once a year. The price is set in yen per kW per year. The left figure shows the clearing prices of past main auctions. The right figure shows the battery and pumped hydro capacity contracted in the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction (a separate auction that supports new builds such as batteries and pumped hydro). Results are published once a year, so they do not change every month. The latest is delivery FY2029 (published January 2026).

The capacity market clearing price in Tokyo was 14,137 yen/kW for delivery FY2024, 3,495 yen/kW for FY2025 and 15,111 yen/kW for FY2029. In the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, contracted batteries and pumped hydro in the FY2025 round were 819 MW of lithium-ion and 886 MW of non-lithium. Like the capacity market, this is the latest once-a-year publication and does not change every month.

Source: Clearing results and capacity securing contract documents published by the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) (sources in the last section (Assumptions and terms)). As published.

Our records | How many months and how much it costs to connect to the grid

A grid connection study is a procedure in which the grid operator checks "can this site be connected, and how much will the work cost?". The grid connection cost contribution is the part of the connection work cost paid by the applicant. Of the response letters we received, we compiled only those for high voltage (connections to 6,600 V distribution lines), by grid area. Extra-high voltage is not included. Grid areas with fewer than 5 cases are not shown.

In the high-voltage-only compilation (as of October 19, 2026), the median construction period in grid connection study responses was 8.0 months in Tokyo, 12.5 months in Kyushu and 17.0 months in Tohoku. The median grid connection cost contribution was ¥3.02 million in Tokyo and ¥32.35 million in Tohoku.

Source: Our compilation of the high-voltage grid connection study response letters we received from grid operators (as of October 19, 2026).

Assumptions and terms

  • The figures are our compilation of the values published in the sources. We have not added up several months or converted anything to annual figures. We do not show revenue amounts for any single battery storage site.
  • Months covered: wholesale electricity (JEPX) is October 2026 (31 days). The balancing market (EPRX) is September 2026 preliminary figures (delivery days up to the 29th). The balancing market month will differ until the published figures for October 2026 enter our compilation.
  • Average prices are simple averages of 30-minute slots (not weighted by trading volume). Daytime is the slots from 8:00 to 16:00, evening the slots from 16:00 to 20:00.
  • Spread: we rank the day's 30-minute slots by price and subtract the average of the 8 cheapest slots from the average of the 8 most expensive slots (4 hours) (4 slots for 2 hours). This ignores whether the cheap hours come before the expensive hours, the electricity lost in charging and discharging, and fees, taxes and transmission charges. It does not show actual revenue.
  • Balancing market: the nationwide figures (total column) of the EPRX preliminary figures. Tendered volume is by grid operator (TSO). Bid volume, winning volume and average winning price are by area of the power source. Volumes are averages per 30-minute slot. The average winning price is weighted by winning volume. Bid-to-requirement ratio = bid volume ÷ tendered volume; clearing rate = winning volume ÷ bid volume. For the 3-hour blocks up to March 13, 2026, we counted one block as 6 slots of 30 minutes.
  • Range of winning prices: the thin line runs from the cheapest bid (minimum winning price) to the most expensive bid (maximum winning price) among the month's slots with winning bids (published values by area of the power source). The published values for Primary reserve (online), Secondary reserve 1 and 2, and Tertiary reserve 1 also include Composite product bids (one price for several products together). So we removed from the thin line any bid above 10 yen that a Composite product bid in the same slot and area can explain. For Primary reserve (offline), we removed from the thin line bids above the price cap (10 yen this month). The balancing market system still clears bids above the cap and cuts them down to the cap at settlement. The thick band is the 10% to 90% range from the bottom when the average winning prices of slots with winning bids are ranked from lowest to highest. The dot is the average weighted by winning volume.
  • Clearing rate by grid area × time of day: total winning volume ÷ total bid volume for the slots at the same time of day across the month (by area of the power source).
  • For each balancing market product, the time from instruction to output change and the duration follow the FY2026 specifications. The online category of Primary reserve connects to the grid operator by a dedicated line. The offline category operates through the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line.
  • High-price slots for Tertiary reserve 2: slots with a nationwide average winning price of 10 yen/kW per 30 min or more. The top 1% is the top 1% of slots ranked by revenue (average winning price × winning volume). The maximum winning price is the published value by area of the power source.
