Battery Storage Market Notes, April 2026 issue

The price of electricity changes with the time of day it is used. "Grid-scale battery storage" (large batteries connected to the power grid that store electricity and release it) earns money from that difference. These notes use published figures and our own records to show this month's movements in figures and tables. This issue is a back issue, made retroactively in November 2026. The grid connection study tally is as of the date it was made (October 19, 2026).

01

The three income streams of a battery storage site (how to read these notes)

A battery storage site (grid-scale battery storage) earns income from three markets. ① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) is a market where you buy and store electricity at cheap hours and sell it at expensive hours. ② The balancing market (EPRX) is a market where you sell grid operators the "capacity" to correct surpluses and shortages of electricity. ③ The capacity market (OCCTO) is a market where the country buys future electricity supply capacity in advance. These notes show the figures for these three markets in that order. The ①, ② or ③ at the start of each section title shows which market it is about.

Source: The rules of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX) and the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO).

02

Three movements this month

We picked three movements this month from the figures of the three markets.

  • In wholesale electricity (JEPX), the monthly average spread between the most expensive 4 hours and the cheapest 4 hours in April 2026 was 23.12 yen/kWh in Tokyo and 13.02 yen/kWh in Kyushu.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), the bid ratio for Primary reserve (online) this month was 74.3%. No month since April 2025 has gone above 100%. The average awarded price was 4.27 yen/kW per 30 min.
  • In the balancing market (EPRX), 60.2% of this month's sales for Tertiary reserve 2 came from the top 10% of 30-minute slots by sales.
03

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | Average price

Wholesale electricity is electricity bought and sold between power companies and retailers. Its price is set on the "spot market" of the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX), where electricity for the next day is bought and sold the day before. "System (nationwide)" is the price when all of Japan is treated as one market (the system price). When the transmission lines between regions are congested, prices split by area from Hokkaido to Kyushu (area prices).

The monthly average of the wholesale electricity (JEPX) System (nationwide) price was 14.77 yen/kWh in April 2026, against 9.82 yen/kWh in the same month last year.

Source: Spot market trading results published by the Japan Electric Power Exchange (JEPX). Simple average of the 30-minute prices. Our tally.

04

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How the price moves during the day

The wholesale electricity price changes every 30 minutes. The figure shows the shape of one day, made by averaging the prices at the same time of day across the month. If a battery stores electricity at cheap hours and releases it at expensive hours, the difference becomes its income.

The daily trough and peak of wholesale electricity were 11.42 yen/kWh and 33.06 yen/kWh in Tokyo, and 3.50 yen/kWh and 17.19 yen/kWh in Kyushu. In both areas, the trough was around 12:30 and the peak was around 18:30.

Source: JEPX spot market. Our tally.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | How large is the spread per kWh

For each area, we took the average price of the most expensive 4 hours of each day, subtracted the average price of the cheapest 4 hours, and show the monthly average. This equals the difference per kWh if you buy 1 kWh during the cheapest 4 hours and sell it during the most expensive 4 hours. It is a simple difference. It does not subtract the electricity lost in charging and discharging or fees, and it ignores whether the cheap hours come before or after the expensive hours.

The area spread between the most expensive 4 hours and the cheapest 4 hours in wholesale electricity was largest in Tokyo at 23.12 yen/kWh and smallest in Shikoku at 9.41 yen/kWh.

① Wholesale electricity (JEPX) | There is a spread every day

We show the same 4-hour spread day by day. The larger the spread on a day, the more room a battery has to earn from it.

07

② Balancing market (EPRX) | Seven products, and prices by area

The balancing market is a market where grid operators buy, every 30 minutes, the "capacity" to correct surpluses and shortages of electricity. The products are divided by how fast output changes after an instruction. Primary reserve is the fastest, and Tertiary reserve 2 is the slowest. Primary reserve has two categories: "online", connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line, and "offline", which runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line. They are tendered and traded separately, so we show them separately. A Composite product is a bid that offers several products together. The price is set in yen per 1 kW of capacity offered per 30 minutes. The figure shows the range of awarded prices by area (by the area where the power source is located). The thin line runs from the cheapest bid to the most expensive bid in the month. The thick band is the price range that contains 8 out of 10 of the 30-minute slots with awards. The dot is the average awarded price. Nationwide figures are in the table in the "Market figures" section.

By the area where the power source is located, the highest average awarded price in the balancing market was 14.57 yen for Primary reserve (offline) in Chubu, and the lowest was 0.37 yen for Tertiary reserve 2 in Shikoku (yen/kW per 30 min).

Source: Trading results (preliminary figures) published by the Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX). Our tally.

② Balancing market (EPRX) | How full are the Primary reserve seats

Primary reserve is the fastest product. It changes output within 10 seconds of an instruction, and batteries are often used for it. The left side shows the online category and the right side the offline category. The bars show the bid ratio (bid volume ÷ volume the grid operators asked for). Above 100%, more bids came in than were asked for, and some bids lose. The line shows the average awarded price. These are monthly figures we have from April 2025. Figures for Primary reserve (offline) start in April 2026.

In the balancing market, Primary reserve (online) had a bid ratio of 74.3% (no month above 100% yet) and an average awarded price of 4.27 yen. Primary reserve (offline) had 30.9% and 12.29 yen (yen/kW per 30 min).

③ Capacity market (OCCTO) | A market that buys future supply capacity

The capacity market buys electricity supply capacity four years ahead through an auction held once a year. The price is set in yen per kW per year. The left figure shows the clearing prices of past main auctions. The right figure shows the battery and pumped hydro capacity contracted in the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction (a separate auction that supports new builds such as batteries and pumped hydro). These are published once a year, so they do not change every month. The latest is for delivery FY2029 (published January 2026).

The capacity market clearing price (Tokyo) was 14,137 yen/kW for delivery FY2024, 3,495 yen/kW for FY2025 and 15,111 yen/kW for FY2029. In the Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, batteries and pumped hydro in the FY2025 auction were 819 MW lithium-ion and 886 MW non-lithium. All are the latest figures from the once-a-year publications.

Source: Clearing results and capacity contract documents published by the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) (sources in the last section (Assumptions and terms)). As published.

Our records | How many months and how much it costs to connect to the grid

A grid connection study is a procedure in which the grid operator checks "whether this site can be connected and how much the work will cost." The grid connection cost contribution is the part of the connection work cost paid by the applicant. Of the response letters we received, we tallied only the high-voltage ones (connections to 6,600V distribution lines) by grid area. Extra-high voltage is not included. Grid areas with fewer than 5 cases are not shown.

The medians of grid connection study responses (high voltage only) were a construction period of 8.0 months in Tokyo and 17.0 months in Tohoku, and a grid connection cost contribution of ¥3.02 million in Tokyo and ¥32.35 million in Tohoku. These figures are separate from wholesale electricity and the balancing market.

Source: Tally of the high-voltage grid connection study response letters we received from general transmission and distribution operators (as of October 19, 2026).

Assumptions and terms

  • The figures are our tally of the values published by the sources. We have not added up several months or converted anything to annual figures. We do not show the revenue of any single battery storage site.
  • Months covered: wholesale electricity (JEPX) is April 2026 (30 days), and the balancing market (EPRX) is the April 2026 preliminary figures (delivery dates up to the 30th). For the balancing market, the month differs until the published figures for April 2026 are included in our tally.
  • Average prices are simple averages of the 30-minute slots (not weighted by trading volume). Daytime is the slots from 8:00 to 16:00, and evening is the slots from 16:00 to 20:00.
  • Spread: we sort the day's 30-minute slots by price and subtract the average of the 8 cheapest slots from the average of the 8 most expensive slots (4 hours) (4 slots for 2 hours). We do not consider whether the cheap hours come before or after the expensive hours, the electricity lost in charging and discharging, or fees, taxes and transmission charges. This does not show actual revenue.
  • Balancing market: EPRX preliminary figures, nationwide (the total column). The volume asked for is by grid operator (TSO). Bid volume, awarded volume and average awarded price are by the area where the power source is located. Volumes are averages per 30-minute slot. The average awarded price is weighted by awarded volume. Bid ratio = bid volume ÷ volume asked for. Award rate = awarded volume ÷ bid volume. For the 3-hour blocks up to March 13, 2026, we counted 1 block as six 30-minute slots.
  • Range of awarded prices: the thin line runs from the cheapest bid (lowest awarded price) to the most expensive bid (highest awarded price) among the month's 30-minute slots with awards (published values by area where the power source is located). The published values for Primary reserve (online), Secondary reserve 1 and 2, and Tertiary reserve 1 also include Composite product bids (one price for several products together). So we removed from the thin line any bid above the price cap of 15 yen for Primary reserve (online) and Secondary reserve 1 (for delivery up to August 31, 2026), or above 10 yen for Secondary reserve 2 and Tertiary reserve 1, that can be explained by a Composite product bid in the same 30-minute slot and area. For Primary reserve (offline), we removed from the thin line any bid above the price cap (15 yen this month). (The balancing market system also awards bids above the cap and lowers them to the cap at settlement.) The thick band is the range from the bottom 10% to 90% when the average awarded prices of the 30-minute slots with awards are sorted from cheapest. The dot is the average weighted by awarded volume.
  • Award rate by grid area × time of day: total awarded volume ÷ total bid volume for the slots at the same time of day in the month (by area where the power source is located).
  • The time from instruction to output change and the duration for each balancing market product are the FY2026 specifications. For Primary reserve, the online category is connected to the grid operator by a dedicated line, and the offline category runs on the simplified dispatch system without a dedicated line.
  • High-priced Tertiary reserve 2 slots: 30-minute slots with a nationwide average awarded price of 10 yen/kW per 30 min or more. The top 1% is the top 1% of slots ranked by sales (average awarded price × awarded volume). The highest awarded price is the published value by area where the power source is located.
