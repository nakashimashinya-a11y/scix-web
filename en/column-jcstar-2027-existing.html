Adding a battery to an existing solar plant: how far does ★1 reach?
— The starting point is the acceptance date of the contract application

The October 2026 draft revision of the Guideline on Technical Requirements for Grid Interconnection puts the treatment of existing plants into the text. It does not apply retroactively. If you add a battery, it applies only to the added side. If you replace a device, the new device generally needs ★1. The starting point is the day the grid operator accepted the contract application.

A series of four. From April 2027, the control equipment of a battery storage plant must carry the JC-STAR ★1 label to connect to Japan's grid. In three lines
  1. Whether ★1 is needed depends on the day the grid operator accepted the contract application. It is 1 April 2027 for extra-high and high voltage, and 1 October for low voltage.
  2. The requirement does not apply retroactively to existing plants. If you add a battery to an existing solar plant, ★1 is needed only for the added devices.
  3. When you replace a device, the new device generally needs ★1. Only in cases of "excessive burden", such as having to replace other devices too, can you submit a plan and get a grace period.

"Our solar plant was connected in 2019. If we add a battery next to it, do we have to replace the solar PCS too?" This is the first question that comes up when a battery is added to an existing solar plant.

In June 2026, when we wrote COLUMN 14, the materials did not say how this was treated. In July, ANRE answered in its FAQ. In October, the draft revision put it into the text. This article sets the line out in one diagram and one table.

The starting point is the acceptance date of the contract application

Whether ★1 is needed depends on the day the grid operator accepted the contract application. It applies to projects accepted on or after 1 April 2027 for extra-high and high voltage, and on or after 1 October 2027 for low voltage.

"Acceptance" is the point when the application documents are complete and the deposit has been paid. It is not enough to have applied for a connection study or received its reply.

Merely having a connection study application accepted does not secure the previous treatment. The previous treatment applies only if you file a contract application on the basis of the study result and the grid operator has accepted that contract application.ANRE, "Frequently asked questions", A2 (our translation)

If your project has "filed the contract application, but the deposit is still to come", the acceptance date could fall after April 2027. Check the date first.

This is not final yet. The public comment period on the draft revision ran from 6 October to 6 November 2026 (e-Gov case no. 620340012). The revised texts of the 10 grid operators have not been published. This article is based on the draft revision and ANRE's FAQ.

No retroactive application

The draft says the requirement is "not applied retroactively" to projects whose acceptance is complete. This holds even if the equipment has not yet been built.

Even if the equipment has not yet been installed, this requirement shall not apply retroactively to a project for which acceptance of the contract application for the power generation equipment is complete.Guideline on Technical Requirements for Grid Interconnection, draft revision, Chapter 2, Section 1, 5 (1) (October 2026) (our translation)

A solar plant connected in 2019 is unaffected as long as it is used as it is.

But even for a project whose acceptance is complete, replacing the PCS or other devices with different ones after April 2027 requires an application for a change to the equipment. The requirement at that time then applies, so the replaced devices need ★1 (FAQ A3).

If you add, only the added side

When you add a battery to an existing solar plant, ★1 is needed for the devices on the added battery side. These are the PCS, BMS, EMS, remote monitoring unit, gateway and so on. The existing solar PCS and monitoring unit need nothing if they are not replaced.

For the covered devices used in an added battery, please use JC-STAR ★1 certified products.ANRE, "Frequently asked questions", A10 (our translation)

Switching from FIT to FIP and the end of the FIT period are not covered either, as long as no equipment is added or changed. If you add a battery at the same time as switching to FIP, only the added battery needs ★1. Even a fully self-consumed plant, which sends no power out, is covered if it is connected to the grid.

The devices covered change with the way the system is connected. Table 1 lists, for each configuration, the devices that need ★1 and the basis. The two DC-coupled rows are not described directly in the materials. They are our reading.

The treatment of DC-coupled systems is not described directly in the materials. Before you rely on it, confirm it in writing with the local grid operator.

A replaced device generally needs ★1

When you replace one PCS after a failure, that one is in principle required to have ★1. Devices you do not replace are not covered.

If, from April 2027, you replace a covered device with a device that has IP communication, the replaced device is required to be a JC-STAR ★1 certified product.ANRE, "Frequently asked questions", A11 (our translation)

Even a replacement with the same type requires a connection study application under Article 79, paragraph 1, item 2 of the Transmission and Distribution Business Guidelines. It does not count as a "minor change".

The only exception is "excessive burden". ANRE gives three examples.

  • Replacing one failed PCS would not match the old PCS or transformer, so the whole equipment would have to be replaced.
  • An extended warranty signed by the end of March 2026 does not allow replacement with a ★1 product.
  • You bought and hold a replacement product by the end of March 2026, and cannot swap it for a ★1 product.

In such a case, you consult the local grid operator and submit a plan with three points. If it is accepted, you may for now replace the device with a product of the same type without ★1.

  1. Why you cannot replace it with a ★1 product (with supporting documents)
  2. When all covered devices will be ★1 (for example, the planned year of replacement)
  3. The cybersecurity measures until then

At a seminar in April 2026, it was explained that replacing even one device might require all the devices at the plant to be ★1 (as reported by Orrick). The FAQ of July narrowed this to the "replaced device". If you replace 1 of 30 units, only that 1 is covered.

Low voltage is October because of stock in distribution

Only low voltage is six months later, because old products remain in stock in distribution.

Even after makers introduce products with JC-STAR ★1, a certain amount of stock of old products is expected to remain in the distribution network for some time. We therefore set October 2027 as the start date.OCCTO, 21st Grid Code Review Committee, Material 4 (31 March 2026), p. 3 (our translation)

The Japan Photovoltaic Energy Association (JPEA) asked for a grace period until October also for high voltage and other systems that use the same PCS as low voltage. Material for the 22nd Committee (21 August 2026) says that after discussion it was decided "to proceed as proposed". The grace period was not widened.

What to check before you buy

  • When the acceptance date of the contract application falls (documents complete plus deposit paid).
  • For a project added to an existing solar plant, whether it is AC-coupled or DC-coupled. If DC-coupled, whether the PCS is replaced.
  • Whether the model numbers of the devices to be added or replaced are on IPA's list (52-2 The list).
  • If you plan to use the excessive burden exception, whether you can assemble the extended warranty contract signed by the end of March 2026, or the papers for a replacement product you hold.

Frequently asked questions

If I add a battery to an existing solar plant, does the solar PCS also need JC-STAR ★1?

No. ANRE's FAQ asks for ★1 in the devices used for an added battery. It does not ask for existing equipment to be replaced with ★1 devices if it is used as it is. ★1 is needed for the PCS, BMS, EMS, remote monitoring and gateway of the added battery side.

If I apply for the connection study by March 2027, do I avoid ★1?

No. The starting point is the day the grid operator accepted the "contract application". That means the point when the documents are complete and the deposit has been paid. Applying for or receiving the reply to a connection study does not secure the old treatment. ★1 is needed for acceptances on or after 1 April 2027 for extra-high and high voltage, and on or after 1 October 2027 for low voltage (under 50 kW).

When I replace a failed PCS, must it be a JC-STAR ★1 product?

In principle, yes. If, from April 2027, you replace a device with one that has IP communication, the new device is required to have ★1. Only where this would be an "excessive burden", for example because other devices would have to be replaced too, you may consult the local grid operator. If you submit a plan stating (1) why you cannot replace it and the evidence, (2) when everything will be ★1, and (3) the measures until then, you may for now replace it with a product of the same type without ★1.

Is JC-STAR ★1 needed for switching from FIT to FIP or at the end of the FIT period?

Not if you do not add or change equipment. If you add a battery at the same time as the switch to FIP or the end of the FIT period, the added battery needs ★1, but the unchanged existing devices do not.
