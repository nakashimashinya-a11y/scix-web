What is the Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction (LDA)?
— A Complete Guide to the 20-Year Fixed Revenue Mechanism

For those considering entry into the battery storage business, this article explains the basic structure of the LDA through the 3rd round policy changes, based on published materials.
In three lines
  1. The LDA (Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction) pays winners a fixed amount from OCCTO for 20 years to cover fixed costs.
  2. Profits from other markets are refunded in three tiers of 95%, 90% and 85%, while the LDA fixed income stays fully with the operator.
  3. Battery win rates were approx. 24%, 20% and 46% in Rounds 1–3; Round 4 bids are accepted January 19–26, 2027.
Update as of November 2026

Under the Round 4 schedule, business operator registration (October 13–16, 2026) and power source registration (October 19–23) had closed as of November 22, 2026. Power source review runs to December 8, and expected capacity registration (December 9–15) is open only to operators that completed power source registration. Bids are scheduled for January 19–26, 2027; the call for bids notes the schedule may change in unforeseen circumstances.

Why the Government Created This System

Japan's power system faces significant challenges as renewable energy expands rapidly. Since solar and wind depend on weather conditions, battery storage systems that balance electricity supply and demand have become essential.

However, large-scale battery storage requires investments exceeding 10 billion yen for construction. For private companies to make such investments, a long-term revenue outlook is essential.

The government created the "Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction (LDA)" in FY2023 as part of the capacity market. Managed by OCCTO (Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators), in a nutshell, it is a system where "if you build and operate a battery for 20 years, the government guarantees your construction and fixed costs."

How the LDA Works — "Defensive" and "Offensive" Two-Tier Revenue

When you win the LDA, revenue consists of two tiers. The auction uses a multi-price format (each winner's bid price becomes their contract price), which differs from the Main Auction's single-price format.

(1) Defensive Revenue (Fixed Cost Recovery) = LDA Fixed Income
Funding to cover "fixed costs" such as construction, personnel, and maintenance. Paid at a fixed amount by OCCTO for 20 years. Annual adjustments based on price changes (core CPI) are also applied, providing a degree of inflation hedging.

(2) Offensive Revenue (Market Trading Profits) = Bonus Profit
Profits from the wholesale power market (JEPX), balancing market, non-fossil value market, etc. Since fixed costs are covered by (1), (2) serves as additional upside revenue.

Even in the worst-case scenario (zero market profits), the fixed income from (1) ensures no losses. This stability is the greatest advantage, making project finance structuring easier and favorably influencing lending decisions by financial institutions.

The Truth Behind the "90% Refund Rule" — Actually a Three-Tier Structure

The LDA has an arrangement known as the "90% refund rule," but it is actually a three-tier structure.

Three-Tier Refund Rate Structure

Tier 1 (95% refund rate): Market revenue up to the business profit margin factored into the bid price. Operator retains 5%.

Tier 2 (90% refund rate): Revenue exceeding the profit margin up to the difference between LDA and Main Auction contract prices. Operator retains 10%.

Tier 3 (85% refund rate): Excess profits above the above thresholds. Operator retains 15%.

Source: TMI Associates commentary article https://www.tmi.gr.jp/eyes/blog/2023/15106.html / OCCTO Detailed System Explanation Materials (September 2024)

Importantly, only (2) market trading profits are subject to refunds — 100% of (1) LDA fixed income stays with the operator. KPMG's analysis shows that the LDA IRR converges to approximately 3.2% "bond-like returns" from capacity payments alone, but the 20-year stable revenue makes DSCR (net CF before debt service / debt service) easier to structure, enabling financing through TK-GK schemes via SPCs (Special Purpose Companies).

Eligible Power Sources and Participation Requirements

Only new construction, replacement, and renovation projects are eligible for the LDA. In addition to batteries and pumped hydro, nuclear, hydroelectric, solar, wind, geothermal, dedicated biomass, and hydrogen/ammonia co-firing (renovation) are eligible, with LNG-fired thermal also included as a temporary measure for FY2023-2025.

Key participation requirements for batteries:
- Minimum bid capacity: 10 MW or more in the 1st round (FY2023 bidding); 30 MW or more from the 2nd round (FY2024) onward
- Continuous operating duration: 3 hours or more in the 1st and 2nd rounds (the 2nd round had two categories: 3-6h / 6h+); 6 hours or more from the 3rd round (FY2025)
- Supply capacity start deadline: within 4 years of winning the auction
- Obtaining a connection study response is a prerequisite for bidding
Source: OCCTO Call for Bids (FY2023 and FY2024) / OCCTO Detailed System Explanation Materials (FY2025) pp.7, 19 / Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, 93rd Working Group Materials pp.21-22

In Round 1, batteries won 1,092MW plus pumped hydro 577MW for a total of 1,669MW. According to KPMG's analysis, approximately 60% of winning battery companies were foreign-owned, showing international investment capital concentrating on this system.

Round 2 saw 961MW for 3 to less than 6 hours duration and 409MW for 6+ hours. The largest awarded source was existing nuclear safety investment at 3,153MW (3 projects).

Warning: 20% Win Rate — Winning is Not Guaranteed
In Round 2, only 1 in 5 bids succeeded. The biggest differentiator is "obtaining the connection study response in advance" and "low interconnection costs." Securing a suitable site is the prerequisite for winning the LDA.

In Round 3, battery bids fell to 2,731MW, about 40% of Round 2. The win rate was approx. 46%, up sharply from approx. 24% in Round 1 and approx. 20% in Round 2. LiB won 551MW against 1,525MW bid (36%), and non-LiB batteries won 700MW against 1,206MW bid (58%).

The solicitation cap was 400MW for each of the LiB + pumped hydro replacement category and the non-LiB + new pumped hydro + LDES category. Each category may be awarded up to twice its cap, and the awarded volumes were 819MW and 886MW respectively (including pumped hydro).

The 19 battery projects awarded are fewer than the 30 in Round 1 and 27 in Round 2, the lowest of the three rounds. The average award per project is approx. 66MW, larger than approx. 36MW in Round 1 and approx. 51MW in Round 2.

Policy Changes from the 3rd Round — Drastic Reduction in LiB Quota and 6-Hour Requirement

Key changes for the 3rd LDA (FY2025 bidding):

(1) Significant reduction in LiB solicitation quota: Total battery/pumped hydro/LDES solicitation cap of 0.8GW. Of this, LiB + pumped hydro replacement is 0.4GW, non-LiB + new pumped hydro + LDES is 0.4GW. A significant reduction from the effective 1GW quota in the 1st and 2nd rounds.

(2) Increase in required operating duration: 3+ hours → continuous 6+ hours. Reflecting policy emphasis on long-duration response to evening ramp-up.

(3) Limit on dependency on specific cell manufacturing countries: Cell procurement from a specific country is limited to less than 30% (supply chain risk diversification)

(4) New eligible power sources added: Thermal with CCS, LDES, new dedicated ammonia-fired

(5) Differentiated business profit rate (WACC cap): A uniform 5% for all power sources in Rounds 1-2, differentiated by lead time to 4% / 5% / 6% from Round 3. Batteries have a 4-year lead time, so their rate is lowered to 4%.

(6) Higher price-cap threshold: 100,000 yen/kW/year → 200,000 yen/kW/year
Source: Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, 102nd Working Group Materials (April 23, 2025) / 104th System Review Working Group Materials (June 23, 2025) / OCCTO Detailed System Explanation Materials (FY2025)

Round 4 (FY2026 Bidding) — Bids in January 2027, Batteries in a Category Separate from Pumped Hydro

OCCTO published the Round 4 call for bids on September 2, 2026 (confirmed as of September 27, 2026). Bids are accepted from January 19 to 26, 2027. Before that come business operator registration (October 13, 2026 through the 16th) and power source registration (October 19-23), and a bid cannot be placed if these deadlines are missed. Contract results are expected about three months after bid acceptance closes.

The solicitation cap is 400MW for lithium-ion batteries (LiB) and 400MW for non-LiB batteries. Pumped hydro and LDES share a separate 400MW category in total. In Round 3, LiB shared one category with pumped hydro replacement, and non-LiB shared another with new pumped hydro and LDES.

A new order of priority applies to LiB. "Certified" LiB, using cells made at the manufacturer's own site by a manufacturer holding a supply assurance plan certification under the Economic Security Promotion Act (OEM not included), is awarded first, and LiB without certification may be awarded only if capacity remains in the category. The cap of less than 30% of awarded capacity per cell manufacturing country applies, in Round 4, to non-LiB batteries.

As in Round 3, eligible batteries are limited to those with 30MW (30,000 kW) or more at the transmission end that can operate at least once a day for 6 continuous hours or more. Since 3-4 hour LiB batteries will find it difficult to participate in the LDA, it is urgent to consider technologies capable of long-duration discharge (sodium-ion batteries, LDES, etc.).

Source: OCCTO Call for Bids for the Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction (FY2026 bidding, published September 2, 2026) p.9 (schedule), p.10 (solicitation volume), p.13 (power source requirements), pp.28 and 35 (certified lithium-ion batteries)

Summary — For the LDA, "Entry = Securing the Right Site" is Everything

The LDA is an extremely attractive system that stabilizes project revenue with 20-year fixed income. However, there are hurdles: an intensely competitive win rate of approx. 20% in Rounds 1 and 2 (approx. 46% in Round 3, when bids fell), a minimum capacity requirement of 30MW or more (from Round 2; 10MW or more in Round 1), a continuous operating duration requirement of 6 hours or more from Round 3, and the prerequisite of obtaining a connection study response in advance.

Securing a suitable site with low grid interconnection costs and a system to rapidly obtain connection study responses — this is the shortest path to winning the LDA.

Frequently asked questions

What does LDA stand for?

LDA is short for Japan's Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction. The government created it in FY2023 as part of the capacity market, and it is managed by OCCTO (Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators).

What is the LDA "90% refund rule"?

It is known as the "90% refund rule," but it is actually three tiers. Market revenue up to the profit margin built into the bid price is refunded at 95%, the part above that up to the gap between the LDA and Main Auction contract prices at 90%, and anything above that at 85%. Only market profits are refunded; the LDA fixed income stays 100% with the operator.

When is Round 4 of the LDA, and how much is open to batteries?

OCCTO published the Round 4 call for bids on September 2, 2026, and bids are accepted January 19–26, 2027. The cap is 400MW for lithium-ion batteries and 400MW for non-LiB batteries, with pumped hydro and LDES in a separate 400MW category.
