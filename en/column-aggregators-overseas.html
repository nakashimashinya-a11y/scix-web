“How do owners choose overseas?” One clue is the league tables.

Overseas, the company that runs a battery for its owner is called an optimiser: the counterpart of Japan’s aggregator.

Most of the numbers in this article come from Modo Energy, a British company that collects revenue battery by battery and publishes benchmarks for each country.

First, the average fell

Britain is on the left, Texas on the right. Currency and units differ, so the bar heights cannot be compared between the two. What to look at is the fall within each.

What paid well at first was the market for balancing the grid. As batteries multiplied and the seats filled, prices dropped.

The way of earning turned over

In the balancing markets, standing by for the agreed hours was enough to earn. As batteries multiplied, the seats ran short. Buying power when it is cheap and selling when it is dear then became the core of revenue.

In Japanese terms, the balancing markets correspond to the balancing market run by EPRX, and buying and selling to the wholesale market.

Then the gap became visible

The yardstick for the gap is the capture rate. Take as 100 the amount that battery, at that location, could have earned by trading perfectly. If it actually earned 76, its capture rate is 76%.

In Texas, each battery’s bidding results are published 60 days later. That is why third parties can calculate results.

Bigger does not mean higher

The September 2026 average was about £97 per kW per year (margin, meaning revenue minus charging cost, from one month annualised). In the same author’s October update (published 11 November), the three largest fleets all finished in the top ten, and EDF and SSE moved up. Rankings move with the month and with how the sample is counted.

The league table is compiled by Richard Howard of the research firm Aurora Energy Research, who publishes it every month under his own name. We found no comparable public league table for Japan.

A league table is not the operator’s skill in pure form. Revenue also depends on where on the grid the battery sits.

What carries over to Japan is the order of events

Pounds in Britain and dollars in Texas cannot be used to check a revenue case in Japan; the markets are built differently (COLUMN 48-17). How far batteries have filled the seats in Japan is covered in COLUMN 47.

In our view, after the second step comes the third, the move to buying and selling. There, forecasting tomorrow’s prices and allocating the 48 slots start to count (Pricing).

Frequently asked questions

Are the results of aggregators (optimisers) public overseas?

In Britain and Texas, third parties publish battery-by-battery revenue and league tables. In Texas, each battery’s bids and awards are published 60 days later. Detailed rankings by optimiser are mostly in paid services.

How much does revenue differ from one optimiser to another?

In Texas in January 2026, of the spread theoretically available, the top quarter of optimisers captured 76% or more and the bottom quarter 27% (Modo Energy). In Britain in H1 2025, excluding the Capacity Market, the average battery earned £69k per MW per year and the best-performing batteries over £100k. The British gap also reflects differences in battery specification and location. The Texas capture rate is measured against each battery’s own location and duration.

Are overseas revenue figures a guide to a battery plant’s revenue in Japan?

Not the amounts: market design and price formation differ. The order of events is a guide. When the balancing market filled, the average fell, earning shifted to energy trading, and the gap between operators opened.
