Grid-scale battery storage is fast becoming part of the infrastructure that supports renewable energy. Yet finding sound projects, shaping them into a form that can be invested in or acquired, and running them in the power markets calls for expertise that spans technology, contracts, the grid and the markets themselves.

With no capital ties to any particular manufacturer or EPC, ScienceX takes on the work from sourcing a project through to planning its operation. We check projects ourselves, fill any gaps through our nationwide network, and deliver them to investing and acquiring companies as confirmed, well-documented projects. As a doctor of engineering, I will keep us rigorous about numbers and technology, with no ties to any manufacturer or EPC.

Representative Director

Founder profile

A grid-scale battery is, above all, a system of measurement and control.

Shinya Nakashima, Representative Director, began his career in 1990 as a researcher at a pharmaceutical company, working on drug quality control and regulatory submissions to the Ministry of Health and Welfare (now the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare). In 1993 he joined Nishikawa Keisoku Co., Ltd., a specialist trading firm and distributor for Yokogawa Electric and Hewlett-Packard covering measuring instruments, analytical equipment and plant control systems, where he stayed for 27 years. There he developed chemical analysis systems and LIMS (laboratory information management systems) and designed GC/MS solutions for analyzing pesticide residues and environmental contaminants. In all, he has spent 30 years working hands-on with measurement, analysis and control technology.

While still employed, he carried out doctoral research at the Graduate School of the Prefectural University of Kumamoto and received his Ph.D. in Engineering in 2015 (environmental and energy engineering). His research on carbon-neutral fuels from biomass conversion and torrefaction later became the technical foundation of ScienceX. Alongside a peer-reviewed paper and a patent, he taught general and organic chemistry for five years from 2021 as a lecturer in chemistry at Tokyo Kasei University. Explaining difficult technology in words that non-specialists can follow is a skill he sharpened in the classroom, and he uses it today when explaining battery storage projects.

In 2021 he founded Science X Inc. The company first developed biogas systems that apply his research at the Prefectural University of Kumamoto, and installed them at three sites in Japan. Its main business today is grid-scale battery storage. What determines a storage plant's quality and earning power is not the cells alone, but the measurement and control technology around them, such as the PCS and EMS. With an eye trained by 30 years in measurement and control, he sources, checks, structures and plans the operation of projects, including extra-high-voltage (EHV) projects of the 50 MW class. The company has no capital ties to any particular manufacturer or EPC.
