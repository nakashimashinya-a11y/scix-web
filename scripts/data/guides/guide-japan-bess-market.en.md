## @tldr

- A grid-scale battery in Japan earns from three markets — the balancing market (EPRX), wholesale power (JEPX) and the capacity market (OCCTO) — on merchant revenue, not a feed-in tariff.
- Grid access is the bottleneck. As of the end of June 2026, 1.28 GW of grid-scale batteries were connected, against about 211 GW of connection studies and about 41 GW of contract applications received (ANRE).

## The market in 30 seconds

- **Three revenue sources.** The balancing market (EPRX) buys the capacity to correct surpluses and shortfalls; wholesale power (JEPX) pays the spread between cheap and expensive hours; the capacity market (OCCTO) pays for being available in a future year. Our monthly Market Notes are laid out in this order.
- **Grid access decides the project.** The transmission and distribution company's connection study response sets the connection point, construction period and cost contribution. Connected capacity is small against the queue: 1.28 GW connected, about 211 GW of connection studies and about 41 GW of contract applications at the end of June 2026.
- **What is for sale.** The projects that change hands are mostly ones with a connection study response, sold either as development rights the buyer builds out or as a completed-build handover.
- **How many projects are for sale right now.** The number we list is recounted every morning, by grid area, prefecture, voltage and expected connection, on [Market data](/en/market-data). It is our own listing, not a statistic for the whole Japanese market.

Sources: Agency for Natural Resources and Energy (ANRE), "Measures for the prompt grid connection of generation facilities including grid-scale batteries", 13th Next-Generation Power Grid Working Group, Document 2 (October 6, 2026), p.3 — [PDF, Japanese](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/enecho/denryoku_gas/saisei_kano/smart_power_grid_wg/pdf/013_02_00.pdf)

## Markets and rules — where the revenue comes from

### Balancing market (EPRX)

The general transmission and distribution companies buy balancing capacity (ΔkW) here, in 30-minute slots. The fast products — Primary Reserve and Secondary Reserve 1 — suit batteries best. From transactions on March 13, 2026, trading moved to day-ahead. The price cap for Primary, Secondary 1 and the composite product is ¥10.00 per ΔkW per 30 minutes for deliveries from September 1, 2026, down from ¥15.00 (and ¥19.51 before March 14, 2026).

Sources: Electric Power Reserve Exchange (EPRX), "ΔkW price caps in the balancing market" (July 30, 2026) — [PDF, Japanese](https://www.eprx.or.jp/information/docs/14b85fbe8942da7d3bdddda80243e7e170f64d9e.pdf)

more: The full guide: [](/en/guide-balancing-market) · Columns: [](/en/column-balancing-market) · [](/en/column-price-cap)

### Wholesale power (JEPX)

JEPX trades 48 half-hour slots a day. A battery charges in low-price slots and discharges in high-price slots; solar pushes daytime prices down and the evening peak pushes them up, and that spread is the source of arbitrage revenue. Monthly spreads by area are in our Market Notes.

more: Read more: [](/en/column-revenue) · The monthly figures: [](/en/report)

### Capacity market and the LDA (OCCTO)

The main auction pays power sources for being available in a delivery year, with contracts of in principle one year, so the clearing price moves every year. The Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction (LDA) is limited to new build, replacement and renovation and pays fixed income for 20 years in principle; a 2 MW / 8 MWh high-voltage project cannot meet its 30 MW minimum, so it runs as full merchant.

more: The full guide: [](/en/guide-capacity-lda) · Columns: [](/en/column-capacity-market) · [](/en/column-lda) · [](/en/column-merchant)

### Equipment — JC-STAR ★1

A Grid Code revision that would require JC-STAR ★1 products (PCS, EMS, gateways and other IP-communicating devices, not the battery cells) is in motion: from April 2027 at extra-high and high voltage, keyed to the contract application. The direction is fixed; the guideline that would carry the rule had not been promulgated when our column was written.

## Grid connection and the site

### The steps

Our development column splits a project into five phases — site screening; land, residents and permits; connection study and contract; design and procurement; construction and interconnection — and puts the whole at least 2 years, typically 3 to 5. Buying a project that already has a connection study response and secured land starts you at phase 4.

On the grid side the order is: connection study → contract application → connection approval → cost contribution contract (within one month of approval) → construction → connection. Three rules shape the timing today:

- A connection study response lapses one year after the response date if no contract application is filed.
- For contract applications accepted from October 1, 2026 (FIT and FIP sources excluded), documents proving the right to use the site are due in principle within two months of connection approval, or the connection reservation is cancelled.
- From August 1, 2026, each transmission and distribution company caps the number of connection studies per company for grid-scale batteries.

Sources: Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO), "Grid access process for generation facilities" (Japanese: 発電設備等に関する系統アクセスの流れ, revised September 2026), pp.11, 14–15 and 16–19 — [PDF, Japanese](https://www.occto.or.jp/assets/access_nagare_20260928.pdf)

more: Read more: [](/en/column-development) · [](/en/column-grid-rules)

### Cost and curtailment

Of the grid connection costs, the construction cost contribution is the item that varies by orders of magnitude with location. Our column names three drivers: distance to the grid, the two-way capacity a battery needs for charging and discharging, and speculative reservation of connection studies. Since April 2023, new connections other than low-voltage ones under 10 kW are non-firm in principle, so output — and for a battery, charging too — can be curtailed when the grid is congested.

more: Read more: [](/en/column-grid) · [](/en/column-nonfirm)

### Site risk

Night-time noise from cooling and PCS fans, flood and landslide hazard, who fixes the DC side when the plant stops, and how the insurance is built are the usual sources of trouble at a storage site.

more: The full guide: [](/en/guide-siting-risk)

## Ways to acquire a project

On our listings a project is sold in one of two forms:

- **Transfer of rights (development rights)** — the land rights and the grid position (connection study response, contract application, cost contribution). The buyer builds the plant.
- **Completed-build handover** — the plant is built by the seller side and handed over with the equipment and the land or land-use rights.

Beyond the two, our columns also cover buying the SPC that owns the plant, and buying an operating plant with a trading record. Which form fits depends on what the buyer can build and what risk it can carry; the stage of the project (study response, connection approved, operating) moves the price more than the grid area does.

## @faq

### Is Japan's grid-scale battery market a feed-in tariff market?
No. A grid-scale battery earns from the balancing market, wholesale power and the capacity market. The Long-Term Decarbonized Power Source Auction (LDA), run by OCCTO, pays fixed income for 20 years in principle and has a 30 MW minimum. A tolling contract is a separate route to a fixed fee: the owner hands the battery's capacity and dispatch rights to a counterparty. See [Capacity market, LDA and tolling](/en/guide-capacity-lda).
more: Read more: [](/en/column-revenue)

### How large is the market?
ANRE's figures for the end of June 2026: 1.28 GW of grid-scale batteries connected, about 211 GW of connection studies and about 41 GW of contract applications received. Not every connection study leads to a grid connection (ANRE). Projects that already hold a connection study response are for sale on Chikudensho Market.
more: Read more: [](/en/column-grid-rules)

### Can a foreign company buy a project in Japan?
Foreign capital does buy projects, but there are gates before the purchase: moving funds out of the home country, inward investment rules under FEFTA, scarce grid capacity and land, equipment, and tax. Our column reads a grid-scale storage plant as a designated sector under FEFTA, so prior notification is likely. Whether and how it applies to your structure is a question for your own legal counsel, case by case.
more: Read more: [](/en/column-foreign-investor)

### What is the difference between a transfer of rights and a completed-build handover?
In a transfer of rights you buy the land rights and the grid position and build the plant yourself. In a completed-build handover the plant is built on the seller side and handed over with the equipment. The gap between the two is mostly construction cost.
more: Read more: [](/en/guide-buy-sell)

### How long does it take to develop a grid-scale battery in Japan?
At least 2 years, typically 3 to 5, from site screening to interconnection, in our development column. A connection study response lapses one year after the response date if no contract application is filed.
more: Read more: [](/en/column-development)
